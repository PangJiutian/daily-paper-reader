ABSTRACT 

Cinematic video generation requires both temporal inference from limited in-puts ( i.e. , sparse rather than frame-by-frame guidance) and factor-aware con-trol of character, scene, action, and camera. Multimodal large language mod-els (MLLMs) offer useful priors for both capabilities, but their representations are not automatically actionable for video generation: a Video Diffusion Trans-former (DiT) may bypass them through native conditioning pathways, and using them does not ensure either factor-aware control or generation-compatible tempo-ral prediction. We propose Factor-Aware Predictive Learning (FAPL) , which establishes effective MLLM latent use as a shared prerequisite and then targets the two capabilities with complementary learning signals. First, latent-only genera-tor grounding trains the DiT with a frozen MLLM and connector, strengthening the DiT’s use of MLLM latents for generation. Second, for factor-aware con-trol , paired videos specify a target-factor change and non-target preservation, and factor-span replacement trains the DiT to realize this relationship in its output. 

Third, for temporal inference , text-only and text-plus-complete-video observa-tions share a video target, and the trained DiT is then frozen while the MLLM and connector are optimized through it. This final stage trains limited-input repre-sentations for generation while retaining complete-observation reconstruction as a complementary training mode. Extensive experiments demonstrate our consis-tently better performance on three cinematic video generation tasks ( i.e. , sparse-to-video, factor-aware editing, and multi-shot generation) over baselines and val-idate FAPL’s contributions to MLLM feature use, temporal control, and factor coordination. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Cinematic video generation (Wang et al., 2025a; Wu et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026b; Yang et al., 2026b; Tang et al., 2026) helps creators explore content, performances, and camera choices before physical production. Beyond visual realism, it requires two capabilities (Fig. 1). (i) Temporal in-ference from limited inputs requires the model to construct plausible action sequences and camera motion from a script and a few visual references (Xiao et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026a), which only sparsely specify how a shot unfolds ( i.e. , they do not provide a dense, frame-by-frame specification of all visual content). (ii) Factor-aware control requires character, scene, action, and camera to follow their respective instructions while remaining compatible: changing a character should pre-serve the requested action, and changing camera motion should not replace the scene (Huang et al., 2026b; Girish et al., 2026). Therefore, the central challenge is not only to infer missing content, but also to make those inferences selectively controllable in the generated video. Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) (Yang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024) provide a promising alternative (Wang et al., 2026): their visual priors and semantic reasoning can help interpret scripts and references, infer plausible temporal details, and coordinate cinematic fac-tors. Recent methods (Yang et al., 2026a; Wei et al., 2026) therefore condition Video Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) (Peebles & Xie, 2023) on projected MLLM hidden states. However, making MLLM features available is different from making them effective for generation. Two obstacles remain: First, MLLM feature bypass is a shared bottleneck for both capabilities: native text-encoder and VAE-reference pathways can reduce the generation loss without substan-tial reliance on MLLM latents (Fig. 2). Second, task-structured supervision is insufficient . For factor-aware control, ordinary video-level training does not explicitly associate an intervention on a particular MLLM span with the intended output change and non-target preservation. For temporal inference, adapting the connector and DiT around a frozen MLLM does not train the MLLM itself to produce temporally informative representations from sparse inputs. Thus, the generator must 2learn how to use MLLM representations, while the MLLM must also learn what representations to produce for generation. We propose Factor-Aware Predictive Learning (FAPL) to strengthen the use of MLLM repre-sentations for temporal inference and factor-aware control through three training stages. First, to strengthen the DiT’s use of MLLM features , Stage I freezes the MLLM and connector and trains the DiT with their projected latents as the sole content conditions, using both text-only and text-plus-complete-video observations. This establishes a generation interface that subsequent stages can use for factor-aware control and predictive learning. Second, to enable factor-aware control , Stage II uses paired videos that change selective cinematic factors while preserving the others. Structured descriptions locate the projected latent spans corresponding to character, scene, action, and camera. Keeping the MLLM and connector frozen, we replace the selected factor span between paired ex-amples and train the DiT against the corresponding video target. The paired videos specify what should change and remain, while span replacement explicitly associates this visual relationship with a localized conditioning intervention, without assuming fully disentangled representations. Finally, to learn temporal inference from sparse inputs , Stage III freezes the trained DiT and jointly optimizes the MLLM and connector using text-only and text-plus-complete-video observations that share a video target. The text-only mode trains prediction from limited evidence, while the complete-video mode provides complementary reconstruction supervision through the same generation inter-face. The fixed DiT directs learning toward generation-compatible representations without simulta-neous decoder adaptation. Experiments evaluate FAPL on sparse-to-video generation, factor-aware editing, and multi-shot generation, with stage-wise ablations examining the respective contributions of strengthened MLLM feature use, factor interventions, and mixed-observation predictive learning. Our contributions are threefold: (i) We investigate the gap between multimodal understanding and its effective use in cinematic generation, and propose FAPL to translate MLLM understanding into actionable guidance for inference from limited inputs and factor-aware control. (ii) We introduce factor-aware supervision through latent interventions, turning paired edits into structured learning signals for how the generator should change and preserve cinematic content, without assuming fully disentangled representations. (iii) We formulate predictive learning through a fixed generator, link-ing limited-input prediction and complete-observation reconstruction via shared video targets to learn temporally informative, generation-compatible MLLM representations. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Cinematic Video Generation. Cinematic video generation (Yang et al., 2026e; Elmoghany et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2026d) has recently received growing attention for producing shots with specified content, performances, and camera motions from limited materials (Wang et al., 2025a; Meng et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026c). Existing studies target the two requirements: (i) Inferring content from limited inputs. Reference-driven and multi-shot methods maintain identity and scene consistency from given references (Xiao et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026a; Liu et al., 2025). (ii) Factor-Aware Control. Camera motion, character replacement, action transfer, and scene modification are typically handled separately (Wang et al., 2023; Tan et al., 2026a; Ye et al., 2026; Jiang et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2026b; Girish et al., 2026; Fang et al., 2026; Huang et al., 2026a). 

MLLM-Conditioned Video Generation and Editing. Recent video diffusion models achieve high-quality synthesis and support a growing range of editing tasks (Blattmann et al., 2023; Kong et al., 2024; Wan et al., 2025). To move beyond single-task systems, recent work couples MLLMs with diffusion backbones, either through in-context conditioning (Ye et al., 2026; Ju et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025) or through an MLLM understanding branch connected to a generation branch (Wei et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2026a; Tan et al., 2026b). Related studies improve controllability over spe-cific factors such as camera motion (Wang et al., 2023) and subject identity (Liu et al., 2025). These systems differ in architecture and task coverage, but commonly inject MLLM features alongside text-encoder and VAE reference features through a shared conditioning interface, and supervise training at the video level. Such supervision does not determine how much each condition con-tributes, and the generator may rely on familiar text and dense visual-reference pathways. Paired editing examples are also typically consumed as additional supervised samples, without explicitly indicating which factor should change and which content should be preserved. Predictive Representation Learning. Joint-embedding predictive architectures learn representa-tions by predicting target features from partial observations rather than reconstructing pixels, avoid-ing unpredictable low-level details (Assran et al., 2023), and extend to action-conditioned future prediction for planning and control (Bardes et al., 2024; Assran et al., 2025). Generative mod-els also decode predicted representations into visual outputs. However, such models usually learn task-specific spaces without high-fidelity video decoders, or require separately trained decoders, and rarely use paired editing data to supervise factor-level change and preservation. In contrast, we reuse a pretrained MLLM and Video DiT and supervise prediction through visual reconstruction with a fixed generator, preserving the decoding pathway while training the MLLM to infer video content from limited observations. 

3 METHOD 

Factor-Aware Predictive Learning (FAPL) trains MLLM representations for limited-input temporal inference and factor-aware control of character, scene, action, and camera. It structures supervision in generation space: factor-span interventions use paired edits to link localized latent changes to target-factor changes and non-target preservation, while shared-target predictive learning cou-ples limited-input prediction with complete-observation reconstruction through a fixed generator. MLLM-only conditioning strengthens the DiT’s use of these representations, supporting both mech-anisms without architectural changes. We first describe the framework and paired data in Sec. 3.1. Training then proceeds in three stages: (i) MLLM-Only Generator Grounding strengthens the DiT’s use of MLLM features (Sec. 3.2); (ii) Factor-Aware Control Improvement trains the DiT to realize target-factor changes while preserving other content through factor-span replacement (Sec. 3.3); and (iii) Prediction From Limited Inputs optimizes the MLLM and connector through the fixed DiT using shared targets across observation modes (Sec. 3.4). All three stages use MLLM latents as the only content conditions. 3.1 PRELIMINARY : F RAMEWORK AND PAIRED DATA 

The generator ( i.e. , Video DiT) must learn both to use MLLM information and to respond appro-priately in a factor-aware manner. We provide these signals through paired observations and paired edits, without introducing a new representation network. 3.1.1 FRAMEWORK : MLLM-CONDITIONED VIDEO DIFFUSION

The MLLM condition is produced through the connector. Two Observation Modes, One Video Target. For each training video x and its description p, we construct two inputs: ut = p and utv = ( p, x ). Both are supervised with the same target video x. The text-only input requires inference from a description, while the text-plus-video input provides direct 5visual evidence. We denote these examples by Dobs . The complete target video is never supplied to the text-only branch. 

Selective Factors Change, the Others Remain. We construct the editing set Dedit through factor-specific selection and generation of source–target video pairs, covering changes to character, scene, action, and camera motion. Each tuple (xa, p a, x b, p b, k ) contains two videos xa, x b and their descrip-tions pa, p b, with only the selected factor k ∈ F = {character , scene , action , camera } modified. Both descriptions are structured prompts: we rewrite each caption into four ordered fields, character, scene, action, and camera, and obtain pb by updating only the edited factor’s field while retaining the others. Field labels and boundaries locate the corresponding token spans in the MLLM hidden states and projected latents, giving each factor an explicit address for intervention. Span lengths follow the text and need not match across examples, and we do not assume the contextualized spans are fully disentangled. 3.2 STAGE I: MLLM-O NLY GENERATOR GROUNDING 

MLLM representations can influence generation only if the DiT can reliably decode and use them. In the original architecture, native text-encoder and VAE-reference pathways allow the DiT to reduce the generation loss without sufficiently relying on MLLM latents. Stage I therefore isolates the MLLM pathway and trains the DiT under both limited and complete observations. We freeze the pretrained MLLM and connector and update only the DiT. 

Mixed-Observation Latent-Only Grounding. For each video x and description p, we use the text-only input ut = p and the text-plus-complete-video input utv = ( p, x ). The frozen MLLM and connector produce Zθ0,η 0 (um) for observation mode m ∈ { t, tv }. In both modes, the DiT receives MLLM latents as its only content condition; the separate text-encoder and VAE-reference conditions are disabled. Dense observations ground the DiT in the visual and temporal information carried by MLLM latents, while text-only observations maintain compatibility with limited-input generation and the text-based interventions in Stage II.

Prediction Through a Fixed Generator. Although the DiT is frozen, gradients still pass through it to the connector and MLLM. Under text-only observation, the MLLM pathway must produce rep-resentations that let the fixed generator reconstruct the target video’s semantic, motion, and temporal content from limited evidence. Under complete-video observation, the MLLM has direct visual evi-dence but must express it through the same connector and generation interface. Training both modes toward a shared video target therefore adapts the MLLM pathway without changing the DiT. 3.5 INFERENCE AND CINEMATIC CONTROL 

At inference, FAPL supports three cinematic applications within one architecture. (i) Generation from Scripts and References. Given a script and optional character or scene references, the MLLM produces latents that condition the DiT through the trained connector and original interface, followed by flow-based sampling and VAE decoding. No complete target video is needed. The DiT can use MLLM latents alone or together with the original text and VAE reference conditions as separate inference configurations. (ii) Factor-Aware Control. We modify only the target factor’s field in the structured prompt and re-encode the edited prompt through the MLLM and connector. We then replace the corresponding span in the original projected latents with the edited field’s span, keeping all other spans unchanged, and condition the DiT on the resulting sequence. We evaluate both the requested change and non-target preservation. (iii) Multi-Shot Generation. Shots are generated independently from their descriptions and available references. Shared character and scene references promote consistency, while action and camera instructions vary by shot.

Test Data. CineFactor-10K comprises 10K single-shot test cases constructed from held-out source groups in CoinVE-200K (Long et al., 2026) and the training dataset released with OmniVBench (Li et al., 2026), with no source-video overlap with our training data. 

Tasks. (i) Sparse-to-Video Generation. We evaluate on 5K cases using text-only prompts. (ii) Factor-Aware Editing. We evaluate on 5K cases, with 1.25K cases per factor. Each case modifies only the target factor’s field in the structured prompt while retaining the other three fields unchanged. All methods receive the same modified prompt and permitted visual inputs through their standard inference interfaces. (iii) Multi-Shot Generation. We evaluate separately on the 100 sequences in the official ShotVerse-Bench (Yang et al., 2026c) test split. Each sequence comprises two to four shots in their original order, with shared character and scene references and shot-specific action and camera instructions. 

Metrics. (i) Temporal generation from limited inputs. Within shots, we measure action sequenc-ing with Motion Order (Zheng et al., 2025) and camera-trajectory fidelity with Translation Error and Rotation Error (radians) (Yang et al., 2026c), using frozen π3 (Wang et al., 2025c) for trajectory recovery. To complement these action/camera measures, multi-shot evaluation assesses character and scene consistency as shot-specific instructions vary, using Subject Consistency, Scene Consis-tency (Wang et al., 2025b), and FaceSim-Arc for facial identity (Yuan et al., 2025). (ii) Factor-aware control. We evaluate single-factor edits using FiVE’s ∩-Acc (Li et al., 2025), requiring both yes/no and multiple-choice checks to pass. 4.3 COMPARISON AND ANALYSIS

5 CONCLUSION 

This work studies how MLLM representations can support temporal inference and factor-aware con-trol in cinematic video generation. FAPL makes these representations actionable by grounding the DiT in MLLM latents, supervising factor-specific changes and preservation, and learning predic-tion from limited inputs. FAPL demonstrates that targeting the right bottleneck delivers substantial gains without added architectural complexity. Our findings further offer insights into how multi-modal reasoning and generation can reinforce each other: reasoning guides controllable synthesis, while generation provides grounded supervision for learning to infer missing content from limited observations. 

Limitations and Future Work. Although FAPL addresses a fundamental weakness of exist-ing MLLM-conditioned DiTs by making MLLM latents more actionable, its performance remains bounded by the underlying models. Current MLLMs struggle to interpret 3D blockouts or reason about complex camera motion for DiT control, while the pretrained DiT limits visual fidelity and complex factor realization, such as realistic acting. Future work may incorporate stronger MLLMs and more capable video generators.
