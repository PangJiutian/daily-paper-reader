Generative World Simulation with DAO and JING
XGEN Team
Abstract
We present a Generative World Simulation framework that couples persistent shared world state with
continuous first-person audiovisual generation. The framework integrates DAO, a computable world
engine, and JING, an Egocentric Interactive Experience Model, to support sustained interaction for
humans and agents. DAO maintains the states of characters, objects, and spaces and their relationships,
advancing the world according to interaction rules and world time while preserving the consequences of
past actions. From this evolving state, DAO derives observer-specific conditions for JING, which combines
these conditions with character, object, and scene references, observation history, and current actions to
generate each observer’s audiovisual experience. Users can explore environments, manipulate objects, and
communicate with characters through viewpoint control and text instructions, with their actions shaping
both the shared state and subsequent experiences. By connecting world-state evolution with experience
generation in a continuous interaction loop, the framework offers an approach to building interactive
worlds that retain their history, evolve through action, and persist beyond any individual observer.

1 Introduction
Video generation models [1–6] have made substantial progress in visual fidelity, motion coherence,
and multimodal conditioning, with recent systems also supporting synchronized audio. Their ability
to synthesize realistic scenes and dynamic content provides a foundation for generating interactive
environments. Building on these advances, interactive world models [7–12] generate subsequent
observations in response to user or agent inputs, while broadening the range of interactions, extending
generation horizons, and improving runtime efficiency. These advances bring us closer to explorable
environments that evolve continuously and respond to their inhabitants in real time. They also motivate
a further goal: recording and updating world state alongside immediate generative feedback, so that
the consequences of actions persist and shape future events and observations. This would connect
exploration, manipulation, and communication into a continuous experience grounded in history and
shaped by action.
Existing approaches explore different aspects of interactive world modeling. Research on game-based
simulation [13–16] studies action-conditioned generation using gameplay data and virtual environ-
ments. Camera-controlled world models [7, 9–12, 17] enable users to explore generated scenes through
viewpoint changes and navigation. Text-controlled world models [8, 11, 18–21] further allow users
to influence character behavior and world events through natural-language instructions. These direc-
tions provide increasingly flexible interfaces for interaction within generated environments. An open
challenge is to organize the resulting changes into an explicit shared world state that can be queried
and updated, evolves according to defined rules, and preserves the consequences of past actions. This
becomes particularly important when a world contains multiple actors, evolves over extended periods,
or is revisited after an observer leaves. Such a system must maintain shared facts and history while
generating each observer’s local experience.
To address this challenge, we present a Generative World Simulation framework comprising DAO and
JING. DAO is a computable world engine that maintains the shared world’s state and computes its
dynamics. JING is an Egocentric Interactive Experience Model that generates what each observer sees,
hears, and experiences next. Actions change the world, and these changes shape the experiences and
subsequent choices of humans and agents. This continuous loop connects perception, decision, action,
and world evolution within a shared world that persists beyond any individual observer. Users can
specify the scenes they explore, the objects they manipulate, and the characters they communicate with,
continually influencing both the world and their own experience through interaction. The framework has
three core capabilities:
• JING: Controllable first-person audiovisual experiences. JING supports flexible combinations
of character, object, and scene references, together with observation history and current actions, to
generate continuous audiovisual content. Users explore environments through keyboard and mouse
inputs and express manipulation and communication intents through text instructions, enabling
first-person interaction across diverse settings and visual styles.
• DAO: Computable and persistent shared world state. DAO explicitly represents characters,
objects, spaces, and their relationships, while maintaining world time and active events. The engine
processes actions according to the current state and interaction rules, carries the resulting changes
into subsequent states, and retains unchanged information. Past interactions can therefore influence
future developments, and world state is preserved as observers change viewpoints, leave, or return.

• Continuous closed-loop interaction in a shared world. DAO and JING connect action conse-
quences, local observations, and subsequent choices in an ongoing cycle. Humans and agents act on
the audiovisual feedback they receive. The resulting changes enter the shared state and shape future
experiences and interaction opportunities. From the same starting context, different choices can lead
to different developments, allowing each experience to build on its history while responding to new
actions.
We build JING by adapting MiniMax-H3 and training on diverse audiovisual data through a staged
pipeline. Bidirectional teacher training introduces camera control, while causal autoregressive training
with resampling forcing adapts the model to continuous interaction conditioned on generated histories.
Memory adaptation and distillation support the use of historical information and efficient generation,
respectively. DAO organizes shared world state through an entity graph, together with actor states, a world
clock, and event mechanisms. It updates this state according to spatial constraints and interaction rules,
with snapshots and event history supporting persistence. The world state is then converted into observer-
specific conditions for JING’s audiovisual generation. This design connects world-state computation
with audiovisual experience generation. This report describes the data construction, model training, and
world engine design, and examines generation quality, action responsiveness, state persistence, long-term
consistency, and runtime efficiency.
Our long-term goal is a persistent shared world in which human and agent interactions have lasting
consequences and world evolution continues beyond any individual observer’s presence.
2 Related Work
2.1 Multimodal Video Generation
Video generation has progressed from text- and image-conditioned visual synthesis toward synchronized
audiovisual generation with diverse reference conditions. Wan [1] and HunyuanVideo 1.5 [2] advance
visual quality, motion coherence, and condition adherence, providing a foundation for generating
complex dynamic scenes. Joint audiovisual models, including SyncFlow [22], Sora 2 [3], and LTX-2 [5],
model visual and acoustic content together to produce temporally aligned outputs. Seedance 2.0 [4] and
MiniMax-H3 [6] further extend multimodal reference conditioning and content control. Together, these
advances enable models to represent scene appearance, character behavior, and accompanying sound.
Building on this foundation, JING combines character, object, and scene references with observation
history and ongoing action inputs to generate continuous, observer-centric interactive experiences.
2.2 Interactive and Egocentric World Models
Interactive world models predict future observations conditioned on actions, extending video generation
to environments that users can continuously influence. MineWorld [13], Matrix-Game [14], and Hunyuan-
GameCraft [15] learn mappings from player actions to visual feedback in game environments. Matrix-
Game 2.0 [17], WorldPlay [7], and AlayaWorld [11] further explore real-time control, streaming
generation, and long-horizon interaction. LingBot-World 2.0 [8] and Genie 3 [18] broaden semantic
control over character behavior and world events. In egocentric interaction, Hand2World [23] studies
gesture-driven object manipulation, EgoSim [24] uses an updatable 3D scene to support continued
interaction, and EchoWM [12] combines navigation with video, environmental sound, and speech
generation. JING focuses on integrating exploration, manipulation, and communication under flexible

2.3 Long-Horizon Generation and Memory
Long-horizon interaction requires models to use generated history within a bounded computational
budget while limiting error accumulation during autoregressive rollouts. Diffusion Forcing [25] and
Self Forcing [26] advance autoregressive video modeling through causal generation and training on
model-generated histories, respectively. To manage growing context, FramePack [27] compresses
historical frame representations, while LongLive [28] combines streaming inference with early-frame
retention. WorldMem [16] and WorldPlay [7] improve long-term consistency through history retrieval
and geometry-aware memory. OmniForcing [29] extends streaming autoregressive generation to syn-
chronized audiovisual content. For sampling efficiency, Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) [30]
and Velocity Moment Matching (VMM), introduced in EditStream [31], reduce the number of sampling
steps through distillation. JING combines adaptation to generated histories, memory utilization, and
few-step distillation to support sustained egocentric interaction.
2.4 Persistent World State and Generative Simulation
Explicit state modeling provides queryable and updatable representations of world facts, allowing world
evolution to be governed by both state and rules. StatePlay [32] jointly predicts visual content and
game states to improve adherence to game mechanics. MASS [33] separates shared-state updates from
observation generation: a learned logic engine processes joint actions, and a rendering engine generates
observations from the resulting shared state. For spatial and entity state modeling, EgoSim [24] maintains
an updatable 3D scene, while Marionette [34] separates structured state prediction, geometric rendering,
and appearance synthesis. Programmable World Model [35] further uses executable programs to maintain
entity states and transition rules, compiling these states into spatial conditions for video generation. DAO
focuses on ongoing character activities and event evolution under explicit interaction rules, translating
local world state into observer-specific text, camera, and reference conditions for JING’s first-person
audiovisual generation.

3.2 Processing and Quality Control
Our processing pipeline converts collected videos into training samples with aligned video, audio,
captions, and optional conditioning signals. We apply common quality criteria across sources while
adapting segmentation and annotation to the temporal structure of navigation, scene revisits, interactions,
and dialogue. The objective is to preserve continuous egocentric experiences and provide consistent
supervision for training.
Source-level filtering.
We first apply automated checks to media metadata and decoding results,
and compute visual-quality metrics on sampled, low-resolution video segments. These checks assess
resolution, frame rate, decoding reliability, sharpness, exposure, compression artifacts, and frozen frames.
Predefined rules exclude videos with severe defects, while cases requiring contextual interpretation, such
as moderate motion blur or potential shot transitions, are retained for further assessment.
Videos that pass this stage undergo semantic assessment using VLM. The model examines frames
sampled across the video timeline, short windows sampled at higher frame rates, and available audio
to assess first-person perspective, motion continuity, shot transitions, visual overlays, and unrelated
background music. The viewpoint criterion requires the camera to represent the observer’s direct
experience, without requiring visible hands or other body parts. Motion is evaluated in context: natural
walking movements and coherent head turns are acceptable, while unexplained jumps or interruptions
that break temporal continuity are grounds for rejection. The model’s structured outputs are checked
against predefined acceptance rules to determine whether each source video can proceed to subsequent
processing.
Temporal segmentation.
After filtering, we use Qwen3.6 [39] to identify candidate segment bound-
aries from sampled video frames. These boundaries are adjusted according to the activity being captured.
For scene revisits, segmentation aims to keep the departure and return within the same sequence. For
interactions and dialogue, it aims to preserve complete operations and conversational exchanges. Each
segment retains its source identifier and original time interval, allowing the processed video to be traced
back to its source.
Processed clips are packaged into sharded LMDB datasets containing video and audio latents, caption-
aligned text embeddings, and applicable camera or reference-image conditions. This unified format
supports data from different processing pipelines and enables variants with and without reference-image
conditioning to share the same audiovisual content. Each record retains its source, temporal interval,
and conditioning settings, preserving alignment and traceability across supervision variants. Figure 3
illustrates the temporal alignment and optional conditioning of multimodal samples across diverse
egocentric experiences.

4.2 JING-Model Training
Using the audiovisual data described in Section 3, we train MiniMax-H3 in five stages to introduce
camera control, enable causal generation, incorporate long-term memory, and reduce sampling steps.
Together, these stages adapt the pretrained model for continuous, real-time first-person interaction.
We first train a bidirectional teacher with the camera-control module described in Section 4.1. Camera-
motion features modulate the Transformer alongside the existing text and visual conditions. We retain
bidirectional attention and jointly optimize model parameters and the control module, allowing the
model to learn camera-conditioned motion from complete audiovisual clips. This lightweight extension
introduces camera control while retaining the pretrained backbone architecture. The resulting model
serves as the teacher for subsequent distillation.
We then train a causal autoregressive model to generate audiovisual chunks conditioned on preceding
content and current user inputs. We augment teacher-forcing training [42] with resampling forcing [43]:
historical segments are perturbed with noise, reconstructed by the model, and reused as context for
predicting subsequent content. These reconstructed histories reduce reliance on error-free context and
help bridge the gap between clean training histories and model-generated histories at inference. Camera
and text conditions can be updated at chunk boundaries, allowing generation to respond to ongoing
interaction.
Next, we introduce the audiovisual memory described in Section 4.1 and train the model to combine the
fixed initial context, a recent-history window, and retrieved memories. During adaptation, we vary the
recent-context length, memory selection, and available memory capacity so that the model learns to use
different combinations of historical information. This provides access to earlier scenes and events within
a bounded context budget: recent content supports local continuity, while retrieved memories supply
cues from beyond the recent window.
To reduce denoising steps, we initialize a few-step student with Velocity Moment Matching (VMM) [31].
VMM matches conditional velocity moments at intermediate noisy states reached during the student’s
denoising process. A frozen teacher and a trainable auxiliary network provide velocity estimates that
guide the student’s updates. This objective aligns with the backbone’s flow-matching parameterization

DAO is a computable world engine that maintains explicit shared state and preserves the consequences

Inputs first pass through action admission, which resolves targets and checks interaction rules and state
constraints, including reachability and target availability. Accepted inputs produce actor intents or

5 Model Performance
We evaluate JING with other video generation and interactive models on WBench [44] and demonstrate its
capabilities in reference conditioning, spatial exploration, object manipulation, character communication,
branching experiences, and embodied interaction. We further examine DAO’s ability to maintain evolving
world states and preserve the consequences of past actions. Together, these results demonstrate XGEN’s
capacity to support continuous interactive experiences grounded in a persistent world.
5.1 Quantitative Results
As shown in Table 1, we evaluate JING on the Navigation and Full splits of WBench [44]. Navigation
assesses interactive world generation across video quality, setting adherence, interaction adherence,
consistency, and physical plausibility. Full additionally covers text-driven subject actions, event editing,
and perspective switching, extending evaluation beyond navigation. On the Full evaluation set, our
bidirectional and four-step autoregressive (AR) variants achieve average scores of 81.0 and 79.9, ranking
first and second among 15 comparison entries. On the Navigation subset, they score 81.9 and 81.0,
ranking second and sixth among 39 entries. These results show that our training and distillation pipeline
transfers the bidirectional model to a four-step causal generator while largely preserving its overall
performance.

6 Conclusion
We present XGEN, a Generative World Simulation framework for sustained human and agent interaction
that integrates DAO, a computable world engine, with JING, an Egocentric Interactive Experience Model.
DAO maintains and updates shared world state while preserving the consequences of actions; JING
generates continuous first-person audiovisual experiences conditioned on references, observation history,
and current actions. Together, they connect actions, state updates, and observations in a continuous loop,
enabling exploration, object manipulation, and character communication grounded in prior interactions.
By coupling world-state evolution with individual experiences, DAO and JING extend audiovisual
generation toward simulation of the world behind it. Our long-term goal is the persistent shared world
envisioned in OASIS, where encounters carry history, choices have lasting consequences, and social
activity continues beyond any individual’s presence. In this vision, humans and agents gain experience
and shape a world that evolves through their actions and continues to develop in their absence. Future
work will focus on improving motion robustness and long-term memory, reducing response latency, and
strengthening consistency between shared world state and generated audiovisual content.
