## ABSTRACT 

Recent 3D world models increasingly use features from geometry foundation models (GFMs) as predictive world states, benefiting from their rich geomet-ric priors. However, the view dependence of these features entangles scene dynamics with camera-induced variation, making the resulting state evolution harder to predict. We propose GeoState, a view-decoupled geometry represen-tation for predictive 3D world modeling that separates scene-state evolution from camera-dependent observation. Specifically, a scene tokenizer aggregates multi-view GFM features into a view-decoupled scene state, which a geometry decoder queries using camera rays. An autoregressive world model then predicts future scene states, while camera motion is modeled separately. The predicted states can therefore be decoded under either predicted or externally specified cameras, while retaining a single context-derived scale throughout the rollout without per-frame scale alignment. Experiments show that GeoState outperforms prior 3D world models on Waymo, supports geometrically consistent cross-view query-ing, and generalizes zero-shot to KITTI and nuScenes. ## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Learning to predict how the physical world evolves is a central goal of world modeling (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018), with direct relevance to autonomous driving, robotics, and embodied intelli-gence. Visual world models predict the future in pixel space (Hu et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024b; Gao et al., 2024; NVIDIA et al., 2025), which can introduce substantial representational redundancy and make geometric consistency difficult to maintain. This has motivated increasing interest in 3D world models, which represent and predict future environments in geometrically grounded state spaces. By explicitly encoding spatial structure and 3D relationships, these representations provide a structured and geometrically grounded state space that more directly reflects the real world. Recent 3D world models have explored a range of structured geometric state spaces, including depth, occupancy, point-based representations, and 3D Gaussians (Zheng et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025; Zuo et al., 2025). In parallel, geometry foundation models (GFMs) have substantially advanced feed-forward 3D perception by learning rich geometric representations directly from RGB observations (Wang et al., 2024a; Yang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025b;a; 2026). Benefiting from large-scale pretraining, these representations capture strong geometric priors that generalize across scenes and tasks, making them attractive state spaces for predictive world modeling. Building on this capability, recent approaches have begun to directly model temporal evolution in pretrained geometry feature spaces (Sun et al., 2026; Lu et al., 2026). In particular, VGGT-World (Sun et al., 2026) treats frozen VGGT features as world states and predicts their future evolution, demonstrating the potential of GFM representations for 3D forecasting. Despite the strong performance achieved by recent GFM-based 3D world models, a strong repre-sentation for geometric perception is not necessarily an appropriate state representation for world 1arXiv:2610.22343v1 [cs.CV] 15 Oct 2026 Preprint dynamics. GFM features are typically constructed from and organized around the observing views, so changes in camera viewpoint can induce substantial changes in the representation even when the underlying physical scene remains unchanged. When such features are directly used as predictive states, a temporal model would account simultaneously for genuine scene evolution and camera-induced variation, although these arise from fundamentally different sources. In particular, even a static scene can undergo substantial changes in feature space as the camera moves, forcing the temporal model to predict viewpoint-dependent transformations that do not correspond to physical scene evolution. This unnecessary variation makes the latent dynamics more complex and therefore harder to predict over long horizons. FR3D (Morbitzer et al., 2026) also shows that separating ego-motion from scene forecasting can improve temporal modeling, but its predictive state still inherits the view-dependent structure of the underlying reconstruction representation. We argue that such separation should instead be built into the representation itself: the state should describe what the world is , while the camera should determine only how that world is observed .To this end, we introduce GeoState, a view-decoupled geometry state learned from pretrained GFM features. Given multi-view observations, a scene tokenizer compresses their geometry features into a compact scene state represented in a shared scene coordinate system, while the query viewpoint is in-troduced only during decoding. Specifically, a geometry decoder takes the scene state together with rays from a query camera and predicts the geometry observed from that viewpoint. Consequently, the same scene state can be queried repeatedly from different viewpoints without re-encoding the scene. This transforms view-conditioned GFM features into a representation in which scene geom-etry is explicitly separated from camera-dependent observation. Building on this representation, we formulate 3D world modeling directly in the scene-state space. The temporal model can therefore focus solely on predicting scene dynamics, while camera evo-lution is modeled independently. At inference time, a predicted future state can be decoded using either a separately predicted camera trajectory or a user-controlled camera. Our formulation also maintains a consistent scene gauge throughout temporal prediction and geometry decoding, avoid-ing the need for per-frame scale alignment during rollout. We evaluate our approach on large-scale dynamic driving scenes, using Waymo (Sun et al., 2020) for in-domain evaluation and KITTI (Geiger et al., 2012) and nuScenes (Caesar et al., 2020) for zero-shot transfer. Our approach outperforms the compared world models in depth forecasting across all reported horizons, while maintaining a consistent scale throughout the forecasting rollout. Experi-ments further demonstrate geometrically consistent querying across viewpoints and generalization across driving datasets. Ablation results show that view decoupling makes the scene representation more predictable. Together, these findings support view-decoupled geometry states derived from pretrained GFMs as an effective representation for predictive 3D world modeling. Our contributions are threefold: • We identify viewpoint coupling in geometry foundation model features as a limitation when using them as predictive world states, and introduce GeoState , a view-decoupled geometry state derived from pretrained GFM representations that separates scene content from how the scene is observed. • We develop a scene-state learning framework that realizes GeoState through a Scene To-kenizer that aggregates multi-view GFM features into a shared scene state and a query-conditioned Geometry Decoder that introduces the query viewpoint only at decoding time. • We formulate a 3D world model that predicts scene evolution directly in GeoState space while modeling camera evolution separately, and demonstrate long-horizon geometry fore-casting, geometrically consistent cross-view querying, and zero-shot generalization across driving datasets without per-frame scale alignment. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 3D W ORLD MODELS 

Recent world models increasingly move beyond pixel-space prediction toward structured 3D scene modeling and forecasting. Copilot4D (Zhang et al., 2024) and ViDAR (Yang et al., 2024) forecast future point clouds, while OccWorld (Zheng et al., 2024) and DOME (Gu et al., 2024) predict scene 2Preprint evolution in semantic occupancy space. DIO (Diehl et al., 2025) represents future scene geometry and motion through decomposable implicit occupancy-flow fields, and GaussianWorld (Zuo et al., 2025) models scene evolution in 3D Gaussian space for streaming occupancy prediction. More re-cently, several approaches directly use learned geometry representations as predictive world states. VGGT-World (Sun et al., 2026) forecasts future VGGT features, while GeoWAM (Lu et al., 2026) learns geometry-aware predictive states for autonomous driving. FR3D (Morbitzer et al., 2026) pre-dicts the latent state of a pretrained 3D reconstruction model while modeling ego-motion separately. In contrast, we remove viewpoint dependence from the geometry state itself and introduce viewpoint only as a query condition during decoding. 2.2 GEOMETRY FOUNDATION MODELS 

Geometry foundation models provide strong pretrained priors for multi-view 3D perception. DUSt3R (Wang et al., 2024a) formulates reconstruction through pointmap prediction, while Fast3R (Yang et al., 2025) scales joint reconstruction to larger collections of views. CUT3R (Wang et al., 2025b) introduces a persistent latent state for continuously integrating observations, and VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a) unifies camera estimation, depth, point maps, and tracking in a feed-forward architecture. VGGT-Ω (Wang et al., 2026) further strengthens this paradigm for dynamic 3D scenes, making it a natural geometry backbone for our setting. We build on its pretrained ge-ometric features and reformulate them into a compact, view-decoupled scene-level representation better suited for temporal world modeling. 2.3 COMPACT 3D R EPRESENTATIONS 

A related line of work compresses multi-view observations into compact scene representations. LVSM (Jin et al., 2025) encodes input views into a fixed-length set of latent tokens for novel-view synthesis, while Surflo (Gu´ edon et al., 2026) aggregates dense VGGT features into a fixed-size global latent representation for efficient 3D surface reconstruction. 3DRAE (Wei et al., 2026) further learns a fixed-length, view-decoupled 3D latent space that can be queried from arbitrary cameras. These works demonstrate that multi-view observations can be represented without retaining a sep-arate dense representation for each view. While they focus primarily on static scene reconstruction or novel-view synthesis, our work investigates whether such scene-level representations can serve as predictive states for temporal world modeling.

## 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Datasets. We pretrain the Scene Tokenizer and ray-conditioned decoder on approximately 10k multi-view scenes from DL3DV (Ling et al., 2024), TartanAir (Wang et al., 2020), RealEstate10K (Zhou et al., 2018), CO3D (Reizenstein et al., 2021), and WildRGB-D (Xia et al., 2024), using ground-truth depth where available and VGGT-Ω (Wang et al., 2026) pseudo-depth otherwise. We then adapt both modules on a subset of the Waymo Open Dataset (Sun et al., 2020) selected for static scene content. World Model training uses approximately 140k windows from 800 segments, each containing four context and seven future states at 0.2 s intervals, with FRONT, FRONT LEFT, and FRONT RIGHT context views. We evaluate on 100 Waymo windows held out from model selection, 113 eligible KITTI (Geiger et al., 2012) drives, and all 150 scenes in the nuScenes (Caesar et al., 2020) validation split. Implementation Details. We pretrain the Scene Tokenizer and ray-conditioned decoder on the multi-view pretraining data for 200k steps with an initial learning rate of 10 −5, and then finetune on the static Waymo subset for 50k steps with an initial learning rate of 5×10 −6. Both stages use a global effective batch size of 36, with the VGGT-Ω encoder frozen. We sample one to five source views per scene and supervise depth reconstruction at held-out query views. For static Waymo sequences, source and query views may span both cameras and timestamps. We then train the World Model in two stages: 20k steps of one-step prediction with an initial learning rate of 10 −5,followed by 35k steps of seven-step autoregressive rollout training with an initial learning rate of

## 5 CONCLUSION 

We presented GeoState, a view-decoupled geometry state learned from GFM features for predictive 3D world modeling. By separating scene evolution from camera-dependent observation, GeoState provides a scene representation that can be predicted more effectively over time and queried from different viewpoints. Future work may extend this framework to embodied settings, where predictive scene states could support perception, planning, and interaction in dynamic environments.

This section provides the implementation details of the Scene Tokenizer, geometry decoder, and world model used in the main experiments. Table 6 summarizes their principal architectural config-urations and parameter counts. A.1.1 GEOMETRY -C ONDITIONED MEMORY AND SCENE TOKENIZER 

Each selected GFM feature level is independently normalized and projected from 2,048 to 512 channels. The four projected features are concatenated and fused by an MLP with dimensions 

2048 → 2048 → 512 . For each spatial patch, confidence-weighted pooling of the dense point map provides a representative 3D point. Its coordinates, normalized by the scene scale, are Fourier-encoded with ten bands and concatenated with log(1 + c), where c is the pooled confidence, before projection to a 512-dimensional geometry embedding. Source-camera conditioning uses a six-dimensional rotation representation formed from the first two rotation-matrix columns and a four-band Fourier encoding of normalized translation. A camera MLP produces a 512-dimensional embedding shared by all patches in that view. The source-ray branch encodes six-dimensional Pl¨ ucker coordinates with four Fourier bands and applies a convolution with kernel size and stride 16, followed by LayerNorm, to produce one 512-dimensional embedding per patch. The feature, point, camera, and ray embeddings are concatenated. A 2048 → 1024 → 512 fusion MLP predicts a correction to the feature embedding; their sum is normalized to form the patch memory. A bank of 512 learned queries of width 512 is updated by eight Perceiver layers, each comprising latent-to-memory cross-attention and latent self-attention. Each attention block uses eight heads, pre-normalization, residual connections, and a feed-forward network with expansion ratio four. A.1.3 RESIDUAL WORLD MODEL 

The four observed scene states form an input tensor of shape 4 × 512 × 512 , corresponding to four time steps, 512 spatial slots per state, and 512 feature channels per slot. Each token is first linearly projected from 512 to a model width of 1,152. A learned mask token is then replicated over 512 slots to construct a fifth state, which serves as the prediction position. To encode token identity, we use learned temporal and slot embeddings of 576 dimensions each. Their concatenation forms a 1,152-dimensional positional embedding, which is added to the projected tokens. Each Transformer block factorizes attention over time and space. Temporal attention is first applied across the five time positions independently for each of the 512 slots. Spatial attention then operates across the 512 slots independently within each time position. The two attention operations are followed by a 1152 → 4608 → 1152 feed-forward network with GELU. All sublayers use pre-normalization, residual connections, and dropout. After the final block, the tokens at the fifth, masked prediction position are mapped back to 512 channels by a bias-free linear residual head. The head is zero-initialized, such that the model initially predicts a zero residual and therefore exactly recovers the Copy-Last prediction. A.2 CAMERA PREDICTOR 

The camera predictor forecasts future FRONT-camera poses from the observed ego-motion and the current FRONT image. The predicted trajectory is used as the future query-camera sequence for both GeoState (Pred. Camera) and Copy-Last (Pred. Camera). A.2.1 ARCHITECTURE 

The camera predictor contains 8.99M parameters and follows a motion-prior-plus-visual-refinement design. Given four historical metric FRONT poses at t − 0.6, t − 0.4, t − 0.2, and t seconds, we first compute the three consecutive relative transformations and map them to six-dimensional motion vectors using the SE(3) logarithm. The motion vectors and their first differences are standardized, projected to width 256, and encoded by a four-layer Transformer with eight attention heads, an MLP expansion ratio of four, and dropout 0.1. Future poses are initialized from a constant-body-twist extrapolation of the latest observed motion. Learned horizon queries attend to the encoded motion features through three decoding layers and predict residual corrections to this extrapolated trajectory. Future time is represented using linear and quadratic terms together with eight Fourier bands. To incorporate scene-dependent cues, the predictor additionally conditions on the current FRONT image. We use a binary motion partition for Waymo. A.5.1 DATASETS AND TEMPORAL SAMPLING 

We construct one candidate window per sequence. Each window contains four historical observa-tions ending at anchor t and ten future targets. Eligibility is determined solely by the availability of the required RGB images, camera poses, and LiDAR measurements, together with timestamp matching. Using a fixed temporal position and at most one window per sequence prevents longer sequences from receiving greater weight. For Waymo, the candidate pool contains one window from each of the 202 segments in the official validation split. We define logical states at 5 Hz and place the anchor at the tenth logical state, 1.8 s after the first complete timestamp. A complete timestamp contains FRONT, FRONT LEFT, and FRONT RIGHT images together with the vehicle pose. The four observations occur at t − 0.6,

t − 0.4, t − 0.2, and t, and the ten targets span t + 0 .2 through t + 2 .0 s. For each nominal time, we select the nearest complete timestamp within 20 ms. We partition the 202 segments into 102 segments used for checkpoint selection and 100 held-out segments reserved for final evaluation. The evaluation subset is sampled uniformly without replacement using seed 20260804, after which its segment identities and anchors are fixed for all paired comparisons. No segment in the held-out evaluation subset is used for checkpoint selection. This fixed sample contains 79 Dynamic and 21 Static windows under the partition defined in Appendix A.4.1. For KITTI, we consider the 151 sequences covered by the KITTI Depth Completion annotations and construct at most one fixed window per sequence. Logical states follow the VGGT-World two-raw-frame stride, with the anchor placed at the tenth logical state, corresponding to raw-frame offset 18 from the first image. We retain a drive when all four historical and ten future RGB, pose, and Li-DAR records are available on this stride, yielding 113 eligible drives. Ineligible drives are recorded as skipped and are not replaced. This common anchor placement keeps the temporal position com-parable to Waymo while respecting KITTI’s native sampling cadence. For nuScenes, we construct one window for each of the 150 scenes in the official validation split. Because CAM FRONT is sampled asynchronously, the anchor is defined as the image nearest to 1.8 s after the scene start, subject to a 120 ms tolerance, rather than as the tenth stored image. His-torical observations occur at t − 0.75 , t − 0.50 , t − 0.25 , t , and ten future targets follow at 0.25 s 19 Preprint intervals. Our method follows the balanced-resolution preprocessing rule of VGGT-Ω. It preserves the nomi-nal patch-token budget while adapting the height and width to the source aspect ratio and rounding both dimensions to multiples of the 16-pixel patch size; a centered crop is applied only when needed to limit extreme aspect ratios. The resulting evaluation rasters (height ×width) are 416 × 624 for Waymo, 368 × 720 for KITTI, and 384 × 688 for nuScenes. Since VGGT-Ω assumes a centered principal point, its pre-dicted intrinsics can introduce systematic errors in viewing-ray directions when the actual principal point is off-center. We therefore use dataset-calibrated intrinsics together with VGGT-Ω-estimated camera poses for source-view geometric conditioning. Architecture. The representation ablation uses an autoencoder of VGGT-Ω patch features. For each training example, the frozen GFM jointly processes four historical timestamps and one target timestamp, with FRONT, FRONT LEFT, and FRONT RIGHT at each timestamp. We retain the target FRONT features from GFM layers {4, 11 , 17 , 23 }; each layer provides a 26 × 39 grid of 2,048-dimensional patch features. Four independent linear projections reduce each feature vector from 2,048 to 128 dimensions. The projected features are concatenated channel-wise and spatially average-pooled from 26 ×39 to 16 ×32 , producing a compressed state Z ∈ R512 ×512 . To reconstruct the GFM features, the state is bilinearly upsampled to 26 × 39 , split into four 128-dimensional groups, and mapped back to four 2,048-dimensional feature grids by independent linear layers. A frozen VGGT-Ω DPT head decodes the reconstructed features into FRONT depth. The compressor has 2.11M trainable parameters.

The compres-sor is first pretrained for 200k steps on the same 9,496-scene multi-view dataset, after which the best checkpoint is selected and further fine-tuned on Waymo for 50k steps. features of the target image and compress them into the latent state Z. The compressor then re-constructs the original multi-layer VGGT-Ω features, which are passed through the frozen VGGT-Ω
