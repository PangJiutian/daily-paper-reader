## ABSTRACT 

Video world models turn actions and camera motion into long visual rollouts, making video a natural interface for interactive simulation. Such interaction requires responsive generation, yet coherent rollouts must retain visual memory of what has already been generated. Chunked video diffusion models usually keep this context in growing KV caches, so later chunks become progressively slower. To address this bottleneck, we introduce Velox, a training-free attention router for region-level context selection using frustum slab correspondences from streaming geometry. Velox represents historical blocks as camera frustum slabs with depth intervals and obtains current-block depth through recency-priority multiwarp. For well-covered regions, frustum slab overlap softly modulates each layer’s model-derived block importance. Uncovered regions retain the model-derived route without geometric bias. The fixed token-block granularity matches FlashAttention-style tiled execution and reduces the attention term that grows with rollout length. Frustum slab construction runs asynchronously with denoising on the same H200. At chunk 48, Velox accelerates attention by 4.36 × and denoising by 2.43 ×. On the full 158-case WBench navigation split, it remains within 0.15 API-free average points of dense LingBot-Fast-Cam. Please see our project page for more details. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Video world models are emerging as a practical interface for interactive simulation. A user specifies an action trajectory or camera motion, and the model rolls the world forward by generating future video frames. This direction builds on rapid progress in video diffusion transformers and large video foundation models (Ho et al., 2020; Rombach et al., 2022; Peebles & Xie, 2023; Blattmann et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2024b; Yang et al., 2024b; Kong et al., 2024; Wan et al., 2025; Ma et al., 2025). Recent works increasingly target controllable long video worlds rather than isolated short clips (Alonso et al., 2024; Valevski et al., 2024; Bruce et al., 2024; Parker-Holder et al., 2024; Sand.ai, 2025; Gao et al., 2026; HY-World et al., 2026). For these models, context management is a central methodological challenge. Past frames carry the appearance, geometry, and object state that future frames must respect. Without access to the right history, long rollouts drift, forget revisited regions, or re-synthesize the same scene inconsistently. The most direct way to preserve this history is to retain an ever-growing visual context. Chunked or causal video diffusion models let each new chunk attend to historical frame tokens through a KV cache. Dense FlashAttention makes this practical at moderate lengths (Dao et al., 2022; Dao, 2023), and causal diffusion or self-forcing training makes long rollouts natural (Chen et al., 2024a; Huang et al., 2025b). The cost, however, grows with the number of generated chunks. Later chunks inherit larger context even when the current camera overlaps only a small part of the past scene. As a result, chunk-wise generation becomes progressively slower exactly when long-horizon consistency matters most. This motivates context selection rather than naive context accumulation. Frame- and view-memory methods select a smaller set of relevant past observations. Context-as-Memory retrieves historical views by camera field-of-view overlap, and VMem stores surfel-indexed views for consistent re-visits (Yu et al., 2025a; Li et al., 2025). Inspired by inverse rendering works (Kerbl et al., 2023; Mildenhall et al., 2021), we observe that regions whose geometry has already been observed should primarily attend to their corresponding past regions. Newly revealed or uncertain regions, in contrast, may require model-derived semantic context and local temporal neighbors. A road block, a facade block, and newly revealed sky in the same noisy chunk should therefore not necessarily attend to the same past views. The question, then, is no longer only which frames to retrieve, but whether long-context video generation can assign each region its own relevant history while preserving local motion continuity and leaving the pretrained generator unchanged. We consider this question at the token-block level in the block-wise execution model of FlashAt-tention (Dao, 2023). Our key insight is that camera frustum slabs with depth intervals can provide region-specific routing signals at the same granularity used by tiled attention. Instead of adding a new conditioning branch or fine-tuning the video generator, Velox summarizes generated history as per-block frustum slabs and uses their overlap to guide which historical KV token blocks each current region should see. For each target chunk, Velox obtains query-side depth through recency-priority multiwarp and scores query and historical blocks by world-space OBB frustum slab overlap. In well-covered regions, this score softly reweights each layer’s model-derived block importance before selection. Uncovered regions retain the model-derived route without geometric bias. Previous-chunk retention and global-aligned Gilbert neighborhoods preserve local motion continuity without a separate cross-chunk seam table. Across 48-chunk timing traces and the 158-case WBench navigation split (Ying et al., 2026), Velox re-duces the growing long-context attention cost while preserving aggregate quality near dense attention. Its advantage grows with the number of generated chunks. At chunk 48, attention latency decreases from approximately 34 .2 ms with dense FlashAttention to 7.8 ms, a 4.36 × speedup. Denoising time decreases from approximately 7.19 s to 2.96 s under the same single-H200 asynchronous setting. ## 2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 VIDEO WORLD MODELS AND SPATIAL MEMORY 

Video generation has progressed from latent diffusion to scalable diffusion transformers and rectified flow (Ho et al., 2020; Rombach et al., 2022; Blattmann et al., 2023; Peebles & Xie, 2023; Esser et al., 2024), with foundation models supplying strong visual priors (Yang et al., 2024b; Kong et al., 2024; Wan et al., 2025; HaCohen et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2025; Gao et al., 2025; Team et al., 2025). Long-video methods extend these priors through progressive generation, self-rollout training, and causal chunking (Kim et al., 2024; Jin et al., 2024; Henschel et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2024; Gu et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2024a; Huang et al., 2025b; Sand.ai, 2025; Chen et al., 2025a; Liu et al., 2025a), while interactive models target controllable persistent worlds (Bruce et al., 2024; Parker-Holder et al., 2024; Alonso et al., 2024; Valevski et al., 2024; Feng et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025d; Che et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024a; Yu et al., 2025b; Decart et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2026; HY-World et al., 2026; Shen et al., 2026). These directions make history management central: more context supports consistency but makes dense attention increasingly expensive. Spatial-memory methods address this tension by retrieving views, preserving revisits, or expanding reconstructed worlds (Yu et al., 2025a; Li et al., 2025; Xiao et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2025a; Chen et al., 2025b; Yu et al., 2024b;a; Engstler et al., 2024). Camera-aware and controllable generation (Yu et al., 2024c; YU et al., 2025; Bai et al., 2025; He et al., 2024; 2025; Yang et al., 2025c; Ren et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2026) and streaming reconstruction (Wang et al., 2024b; Leroy et al., 2024; Cabon et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025a; Wang et al., 2024a; 2025a;b; Keetha et al., 2025) further make projectable memory practical. Velox uses this memory to route pretrained attention rather than adding a conditioning pathway or retrained memory module. 2.2 EFFICIENT ATTENTION AND CONTEXT ROUTING 

Long-context transformers reduce attention cost through sparsity, locality, compression, state reuse, heavy-hitter selection, and KV eviction (Kitaev et al., 2020; Zaheer et al., 2020; Beltagy et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2020; Dai et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2023b; Kwon et al., 2023; Li & Wang, 2026), while FlashAttention is the dense IO-aware baseline (Dao et al., 2022; Dao, 2023). Video accelerators additionally exploit spatial, temporal, denoising-step, and feature regularity through adaptive sparsity, caching, and quantized attention (Zhang et al., 2025c; Xi et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025b; Zhang et al., 2025b; Xia et al., 2025; Ding et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2024; 2025a; Liu et al., 2024b; 2025b; 2024a; Guo et al., 2024; Nawaz et al., 2026; Feng et al., 2026). These approaches usually derive sparsity from position, cached features, or attention statistics, whereas frame-memory methods select views but retain dense attention within them. Velox connects both directions by using projective correspondence as a soft prior for region-specific attention routes, with model-derived fallback and temporal safeguards. 

## 3 METHOD 

Overview. Long-horizon generation requires persistent visual history, yet dense KV attention makes every current region process all retained blocks. Velox keeps the pretrained generator and conditioning interface unchanged and modifies only the inference-time visibility relation between current query blocks and historical KV blocks. It represents history as camera frustum slabs with depth intervals, 3Preprint uses their overlap as a soft prior over each layer’s model-derived block importance, and exposes each current region to a compact route. ## 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS 

We instantiate Velox on the LingBot-Fast-Cam inference stack (Gao et al., 2026) with a Wan-style video diffusion transformer. The reported configuration uses recency-priority multiwarp Q-depth, world-space OBB frustum slab IoU, grouped fragment aggregation, and global-aligned Gilbert KV storage. We apply fixed soft geometric fusion to well-covered rows at every transformer layer, with 

τ = 0 .1, gref = 0 .2, λ = 2 , and ηcov = 0 .9. Covered rows retain Kcov = 80 blocks. Uncovered rows use the model-derived cumulative-mass route with Kunc = 150 and no route-size floor. 7Preprint The measured sparse path is a native Triton indexed block-sparse attention implementation rather than a dense call with a Python mask. Current queries are reordered once per chunk, KV caches are stored in global-aligned Gilbert order, and the kernel consumes routed block lists with a runtime route-length bound. The previous chunk is always retained within the route budget, while current-chunk local neighbors and reference-frame blocks act as deterministic safeguards. Frustum slab state is prepared asynchronously with a two-chunk freshness lag, so chunk c normally consumes geometry through chunk c − 2. In the reported timing traces, DiT denoising and asynchronous geometry construction run concurrently on one H200. We evaluate quality on all 158 cases of the WBench navigation split (Ying et al., 2026). The three LingBot-based rows use identical generated cases and the Gemini judge for VLM-scored metrics. Published WBench rows use the official Doubao judge, so judge-dependent values should not be compared across these two groups. ## 5 CONCLUSION 

We present Velox, a training-free router that projects spatial memory into the current latent camera view and uses frustum slab overlap to modulate layer-specific block importance while retaining model-derived fallback and local temporal safeguards. At chunk 48, Velox accelerates attention by 

4.36 × and denoising by 2.43 × with asynchronous geometry construction on one H200. Across all 158 WBench navigation cases, it remains within 0.15 API-free average points of dense LingBot-Fast-Cam and slightly improves the 19-metric overall average. B.4 RUNTIME PATH AND TIMING SCOPE 

The measured sparse path is a Triton indexed block-sparse attention implementation, not a Python masking wrapper around dense attention. Queries are reordered once per chunk into the global-aligned Gilbert token order, and KV caches are written directly in that layout so that the hot kernel loads contiguous KV tiles without a per-call host permutation. The kernel supports rectangular current-query versus growing-history attention, consumes the routed block lists produced by the fused-score scheduler, and uses a runtime route-length bound so later chunks do not trigger new compiler specializations. Section D gives the full kernel path. Frustum slab geometry is built asynchronously. After a chunk is decoded, a worker thread on a dedicated CUDA stream updates reconstruction state, records chunk cameras, builds stored frustum slabs, refreshes multiwarp frame caches, and prepares the Velox state for a later chunk while the main stream continues DiT denoising. The implementation uses a two-chunk freshness lag. Chunk c

normally consumes geometry through chunk c − 2, which is sufficient because frustum slab overlap changes gradually. The worker is the sole writer to the geometry objects. The main stream waits only on a completion event when a future state is needed. ## C ASYNCHRONOUS FRUSTUM SLAB CONSTRUCTION 

The geometry pipeline would be expensive if it were inserted serially between generated chunks. In a synchronous implementation, chunk c must be denoised, decoded, reconstructed into depth and pose state, inserted into the frustum slab table and multiwarp cache, aligned with the next target cameras, and scored before chunk c + 1 can denoise. The reconstruction and frustum slab preparation path is on the same order as one DiT denoise step in our setting, so serial execution would hide the attention-kernel gains. Velox relaxes this dependency with a two-chunk freshness lag. Target chunk c consumes a Velox state built from frustum slabs through chunk c − 2, while the worker prepares states for later chunks. The state remains self-consistent: each worker task first inserts exactly one decoded chunk into the frustum slab table and multiwarp cache, then scores that table against the target chunk’s latent cameras. Chunks before the first available Velox state run dense attention, so the method never consumes an uninitialized route. The implementation uses one Python worker thread and one dedicated CUDA stream. The worker is the sole writer to the frustum slab table, pose aligner, multiwarp frame cache, and sparse-attention context. The main denoising thread submits a geometry task after a chunk decode and receives a future keyed by the target chunk. Immediately before chunk c denoises, the main stream waits on the future’s CUDA event if the worker has not already finished, then consumes the prepared geometric and importance states. This wait is a device-side stream dependency. There is no host-wide synchronization in the hot path. Each geometry task performs four operations. First, the reconstructed frames are passed through the streaming reconstruction backend to update depths, poses, confidence, and masks. Second, the relative alignment maps target camera-control poses into the reconstructed frame and refreshes stored slab cameras under the current bridge. Third, valid depth samples are converted into per-block frustum slab primitives and inserted into the versioned frustum slab table, while first and last frame depths are cached for multiwarp. Fourth, the sparse context warps cached depths into the target cameras, builds Q frustum slabs, scores OBB overlap against historical frustum slabs, computes coverage, and materializes the geometric state consumed by soft fusion at each attention layer. The implementation also moves VAE decode off the denoise critical path with a side stream, prewarms the worker thread’s cuDNN handle, prewarms segmentation and sparse-attention kernels outside the measured interval, and skips frustum slab scoring for the final chunk because no later denoise consumes it. Frames are moved to CPU after decode and copied back on the worker stream, avoiding extra GPU residency while the main stream denoises. All reported timing results use a single NVIDIA H200. The async worker and the DiT denoiser share that one GPU. Memory reconstruction is not offloaded to a second device. A second GPU could reduce CUDA-stream contention and further improve end-to-end time, but it is not part of the reported measurement protocol.

D.5 FAILURE MODES AND VALIDATION SCOPE 

Velox is most reliable when the frustum slab table and multiwarp Q-depth explain the current view. The expected failure cases are therefore geometry-driven. If reconstructed depth drifts, if the multiwarp cache fills a newly disoccluded token with stale far geometry, or if OBB approximation overestimates overlap for a thin structure, soft fusion can promote stale or misleading history. The current router mitigates these cases by applying geometry only to well-covered rows, bounding the geometric multiplier, retaining the layer-specific model distribution, and preserving previous-chunk and local-neighbor safeguards. The goal is to remove history that is spatially irrelevant while retaining enough model evidence for noisy generated-video geometry. The WBench evaluation also suggests where future quality regressions should be inspected. Velox improves spatial and photometric consistency over dense attention, but perspective and subject consistency decrease. This is consistent with the design risk: static background regions are usually supported by frustum slabs, while object- or camera-sensitive regions can be dynamic, partially reconstructed, or semantically important despite weak geometric correspondence. A stronger quality study should therefore report object-centric cases separately from background revisits and regions where Q-depth or frustum slab overlap assumptions are stressed. Finally, speed and quality should be validated under matched budgets. A random same-size route can test whether any sparsity helps. A recent-window route can test whether temporal locality is enough. A frame/FOV route can test whether view-level retrieval is sufficient. A model-derived route can test whether geometry adds value beyond attention statistics. Velox should outperform these baselines specifically when current regions have different relevant histories. This validation target is sharper than only comparing against dense attention, because it isolates the contribution of frustum slab geometry as the routing signal. 22
