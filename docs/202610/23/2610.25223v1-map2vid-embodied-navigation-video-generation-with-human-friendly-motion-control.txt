Abstract —Controllable video generation has recently achieved impressive visual quality, yet existing control paradigms remain poorly suited to embodied navigation. Text prompts are spatially ambiguous, while camera-trajectory control relies on low-level motion specifications that are unintuitive for human users and difficult to manipulate. In this work, we introduce Map2Vid, a novel task that reformulates embodied navigation video generation as a BEV-map-to-video synthesis problem, together with a large-scale benchmark for systematic evaluation. By using BEV maps, which provide a visual representation of global scene layout and intended navigation routes, Map2Vid enables human-friendly navigation video generation. To support this task, we construct a dedicated dataset with over 100K navigation sequences across 61 indoor scenes, each paired with egocentric videos, BEV maps, camera trajectories and language instructions. We further develop a unified framework that integrates multiple control signals, together with a curriculum training strategy that progressively weakens non-map inputs, enabling flexible inference under both full conditioning and map-only input. I. I NTRODUCTION 

Embodied navigation [1], [2], [3] requires an agent to perceive the world from an egocentric perspective and move through an environment under the geometric constraints im-posed by scene layout. Beyond its importance to Embodied AI, this setting also exposes a distinctive challenge for controllable video generation: the synthesized video must remain temporally coherent [4] under long-horizon ego-motion, while preserving scene geometry across turns, corridor traversals, and room transitions. Compared with generic text-to-video generation, navigation video synthesis therefore demands stronger spatial grounding, more reliable temporal consistency, and a control interface that can specify motion in an interpretable way. Recent progress in large-scale video generation has sub-stantially improved visual quality and motion realism. Most state-of-the-art approaches [5], [6] condition the generation on text prompts and an initial frame, where textual descriptions primarily emphasize the appearance of the scene and cannot provide spatial grounding. This makes it difficult to maintain arXiv:2610.25223v1 [cs.CV] 18 Oct 2026 JOURNAL OF L ATEX CLASS FILES, VOL. 18, NO. 9, SEPTEMBER 2020 2

trajectory consistency and geometric plausibility over long horizons. Several recent works [7], [8] attempt to address this limitation by introducing explicit camera trajectory con-trol. Although effective in certain scenarios, they still suffer from two fundamental limitations: First, they support only local, small-scale camera motions (modest translations and rotations). In contrast, embodied navigation involves large-scale cumulative pose changes, including frequent large-angle turns and long-range transitions across rooms, resulting in a substantial mismatch in motion scale. Second, trajectory-based control depends on low-level specifications such as camera transformation matrices. Such representations offer limited abstraction over the spatial structure of the environment, and are fundamentally misaligned with how humans naturally express navigation intent, making them difficult for non-expert use. These limitations motivate us to revisit the control signal itself. In natural navigation, humans rarely reason in terms of camera matrices; instead, they reason about the scene layout and the route through it .For this reason, we introduce 

Map2Vid , a new task that reformulates embodied navigation video generation as a BEV map. As shown in Fig. 1, Map2Vid takes as input a BEV map depicting the scene layout together with an intended navigation route, and aims to generate an egocentric video consistent with the specified path. Compared to text prompt or camera trajectory, BEV map provides structurally grounded and globally consistent control signals, enabling a more human-friendly control interface for navigation video generation. To instantiate this task, we construct a large-scale dataset Map2Vid-100K . Specifically, we leverage high-fidelity indoor environments of Matterport3D [9], and sample valid navigation trajectories from the R2R benchmark [2]. Each navigation episode is executed in the Habitat-Sim [10] simulator, during which we record the corresponding egocentric video together with aligned natural language instruction, camera trajectories and BEV map. The dataset covers 61 indoor scenes and contains 117.8K navigation sequences, with 94.2K used for training and 23.6K reserved for evaluation. While our benchmark is constructed in simulation, it provides precise geometric alignment across modalities, which is difficult to obtain in real-world settings. Building on this dataset, we adapt several representative video generation models to support map-conditioned navigation video synthesis. During training, models are provided with multiple control signals, including the BEV map, camera trajectories, text instruction, and initial frame. To prevent models from over-relying on expressive but less human-friendly controls, we propose a curriculum training strategy that gradually weakens non-map conditions, encouraging the model to internalize the spatial dynamics semantics encoded in the BEV map. This design enables flexible inference at test time, supporting either full control signals or the BEV map alone. Extensive experiments demonstrate that controllable navigation video generation via BEV map is viable and promising, while also highlighting the intrinsic difficulty of the task. In summary, our contributions are threefold: (1) We introduce Map2Vid, a novel task that reformulates embodied navigation video generation as a BEV map, providing a human-friendly control interface. (2) We construct Map2Vid-100K, a large-scale benchmark comprising over 117K synchronized multimodal navigation sequences (videos, BEV maps, trajectories, instruc-tions) across 61 scenes, establishing a standardized dataset and dual-aspect evaluation protocol for this new task. (3) We provide a systematic empirical study across four state-of-the-art video foundation models and propose a unified baseline framework with a progressive curriculum training strategy, validating the feasibility of the task and laying a strong empirical foundation for future research. II. R ELATED WORK 

Controllable Video Generation. Recent advances in video generation have been significantly driven by scalable Diffusion Transformers (DiT) [11]. Prominent examples of this architec-ture include flagship models such as HunyuanVideo [6], [12] and Wan [5]. Despite these successes, achieving fine-grained controllability in video generation remains challenging. Early approaches adapted image-based conditioning techniques, such as ControlNet [13] and T2I-Adapter [14], to the temporal dimension for structural guidance [15], [16], [17]. While these methods effectively enforce spatial structures (e.g., Canny edges, depth maps, or pose skeletons), they often struggle to maintain long-horizon temporal coherence and 3D geometric consistency. To better control motion dynamics, subsequent works (e.g., MotionCtrl [18], DragNUWA [19] and CameraCtrl [7]) introduce explicit camera trajectory-based motion conditioning, with CameraCtrl [7] standing out with its Plucker embeddings. Though these methods enable the geometry control of the video generation, the input form of accurate camera trajectories is too non-trivial for normal users. By contrast, in this paper, we for the first time introduce and explore the BEV map as a geometric condition. 

Embodied Video Understanding & Synthesis. Advanced by large-scale egocentric datasets such as Ego4D [20] and Ego-Exo4D [21], the field has expanded from passive video under-standing to agent-centric embodied world models (e.g., GAIA-1 [22], UniSim [23]). In the realm of embodied video synthesis, existing works primarily address geometric consistency through trajectory conditioning in structured driving domains [24], [25] or via cross-view synthesis from third-person observations [26]. Distinct from these approaches, the potential of top-down BEV maps as a spatially grounded control signal remains largely underexplored. Embodied AI has increasingly shifted from passive perception toward agent-centric understanding, supported by large-scale egocentric datasets such as Ego4D [20] and Ego-Exo4D [21]. Beyond understanding, recent work has explored video generation as a foundation for embodied world models that simulate agent–environment interactions, exemplified by GAIA-1 [22] and UniSim [23]. A central challenge in this line of research is generating egocentric video that remains consistent with agent motion and scene geometry over long horizons. Several studies address this chal-lenge through trajectory-conditioned generation, particularly in structured domains such as autonomous driving, where motion patterns and scene layouts are relatively constrained (e.g., DriveDreamer [25], MagicDrive [24]). A. Task Formulation 

The primary objective of the Map2Vid task is to enable the synthesis of embodied egocentric navigation videos conditioned on a BEV map, supplementing conventional modalities such as images and text, as well as the recently explored camera trajectory modality. Formally, let V ∈ RT ×H×W ×3 denote a target video sequence of length T , representing an agent’s first-person view navigating through a scene. We define the conditional generation problem as modeling the probability distribution p(V|C ), where C represents the set of control conditions. To fully leverage all available and complementary signals, we define C to include the following four modalities: 1) BEV Map ( m): A bird’s-eye-view image of the environ-ment, overlaid with a graphical depiction of the intended navigation route. 2) Navigation Instruction ( q): A natural language descrip-tion of the path (e.g.,“Walk past the kitchen...”). 3) Camera Trajectory ( τ ): A sequence of camera pa-rameters τ = {pt}Tt=1 , where each pt ∈ R16 fully parameterizes the projection model of the t-th frame. Specifically, pt is formed by concatenating the intrinsic parameter vector k = [ fx, f y , c x, c y ]⊤ and the row-major flattened extrinsic matrix Mext = [ R|t] ∈ R3×4.4) Reference Frame ( x0): The initial observation.

B. Map2Vid Benchmark 

To facilitate the Map2Vid task, we introduce Map2Vid-100K, a large-scale multimodal dataset consisting of aligned egocentric videos, BEV maps, language instructions, camera trajectories, and reference frames. It is constructed by extending the Room-to-Room (R2R) benchmark [2] within the Habitat simulator [10] through two steps: 

① Scene Curation and Trajectory Generation: The R2R benchmark provides a large collection of language-guided navi-gation trajectories grounded in Matterport3D [9] environments, making it well suited for embodied navigation data construction. Specifically, we employ the Habitat simulator to re-simulate the training split of the R2R dataset, preserving the original navigation instructions and action sequences. To support our scene-by-scene fine-tuning setting, we treat different floors within the same scan as independent scenes and discard trajectories involving cross-floor transitions, ensuring single-level geometric consistency. Within each curated scene, an agent executes the ground-truth actions to generate navigation episodes. 

② Synchronized Data Acquisition: During execution, we syn-chronously record the multimodal data tuple {V , m, q, τ , x0}.Specifically, camera trajectories τ are obtained by logging agent kinematics. The egocentric videos V and initial frames 

x0 are rendered based on τ , while the text instructions q are inherited from R2R annotations. Finally, the BEV maps m

are generated by projecting the 3D scene geometry into 2D occupancy maps and overlaying the executed navigation routes as a graphical trajectory. JOURNAL OF L ATEX CLASS FILES, VOL. 18, NO. 9, SEPTEMBER 2020 4

Benchmark Statistics. As illustrated in Fig. 2, Map2Vid-100K comprises 117.8K navigation episodes spanning 61 indoor scenes, with 94.2K for training and 23.6K for testing. The generated egocentric videos have an average of 65 frames, sampled at 10 Hz, amounting to 211.6 hours of video in total. Trajectories have an average length of 10.5 meters (Std. 15.7 m), covering short segments to long-horizon paths with substantial ego-motion. 

C. Evaluation Metrics 

To evaluate the generated videos comprehensively, we propose a series of evaluation metrics, encompassing both visual quality and geometric consistency assessment. 

Visual Quality Metrics. We report standard video generation metrics to assess the realism and perceptual quality. Specifically, 

Fr´ echet Video Distance (FVD) [27] measures distributional discrepancy at the temporal level, and Fr´ echet Inception Distance (FID) [28] evaluates the frame level visual quality. In addition, LPIPS [29] is used to quantify the perceptual similarity.

Specifically, we apply VGG-SfM [30] to recover an estimated camera trajectory ˆτ = {ˆpt}Tt=1 from ˆV, and then compare it with the ground truth trajectory τ = {pt}Tt=1 . C. Curriculum Training with Progressive Masking

We build our method on four DiT-based video generation models: Wan2.2-5B-Camera Control [5], Hunyuan Video v1.5 (8.3B) [6], CogVideoX v1.5 (5B) [37], and OpenSora v1.3 (1B) [38]. For curriculum train-ing, we run the optimization for 2000 iterations, where the first 10% is used for warmup and the last 10% for cooldown. a) Evaluation Metrics.: We assess performance across two dimensions: (1) Visual Quality: We report FID , FVD ,and LPIPS to measure photorealism and temporal coherence. (2) Geometric Consistency: To verify embodied navigation capabilities, we reconstruct 3D trajectories from generated videos using VGGSfM [30]. We measure the SfM Success Rate (SfM-SR) , Absolute Pose Error (APE) , and Rotation Error (APE-Rot) between the input and reconstructed trajectories. 

B. Main Results 

We select 6 representative indoor scenes with substantial diversity including church, office, villa, basement, hotel, and restaurant from the Map2Vid-100K dataset with a per-scene training protocol to enable controlled analysis, following common practice in simulation-based embodied settings[39]. For evaluation, we generate 50 videos per scene per model under two inference configurations: full conditioning (All) and map-only conditioning (BEV). Tab. I reports the quantitative results. We analyze these results from multiple perspectives below. 

All vs. BEV Map-only Conditioning. Inference with the full set of control signals consistently outperforms that relies solely on the BEV map across all backbones and metrics. This indicates that auxiliary conditions play a crucial role in stabilizing generation and improving both visual quality and temporal coherence. At the same time, it highlights the inherent difficulty of the Map2Vid task when rich semantic guidance and explicit low-level motion cues (camera trajectories) are absent. Notably, Wan exhibits the smallest performance drop among all backbones, with especially pronounced advantages on motion-related metrics (e.g., SfM-SR). A plausible explanation is Wan2.2-Camera’s trajectory-based pretraining, which equips it with stronger motion and geometric priors and thus enables it to better leverage the control signals from the BEV map. 

Model-wise Performance. From a model-centric perspective, Wan and Hunyuan achieve the strongest overall performance across metrics. This result is consistent with their performance in general video generation, suggesting that the visual and temporal priors learned through large-scale generic video pretraining remain highly beneficial for embodied navigation video generation. 

C. Ablation Study 

Inference with Different Control Signals. Following the same experimental setup as in Sec. V-B, Tab. II evaluates the effects of different combinations of control signals during the inference stage. Several observations emerge from the results: ① Full conditioning achieves the best performance on five out of six metrics, including both distributional visual metrics and all three geometric metrics, while yielding a slightly higher LPIPS than some reduced-condition configurations, indicating that semantic cues (instruction and first frame) and geometric cues (BEV map and trajectory) are heterogeneous geometric cues. ②

Adding the BEV map and camera trajectory improves motion-related metrics. The BEV map is a stronger standalone control signal than the trajectory, suggesting that it provides richer and more stable global spatial constraints, whereas trajectory-only conditioning lacks sufficient context and is prone to geometric drift. ③ Semantic cues are critical for perceptual quality. Removing the instruction and first frame leads to a pronounced degradation in perceptual metrics, highlighting their role as strong anchors for appearance and semantic consistency.

L IMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK 

While Map2Vid establishes a robust baseline for map-conditioned navigation video generation, several limitations warrant further investigation. Our study is conducted in a controlled simulated setting. The current benchmark is built by replaying R2R trajectories in Habitat over Matterport3D scenes, which enables precise synchronization between routed BEV maps, egocentric videos, language instructions, and camera trajectories. This design is important for task formulation and reproducible evaluation, but it also means that our conclusions are limited to a simulation-based benchmark rather than real-world deployment. In particular, appearance statistics, rendering artifacts, and motion dynamics in simulation may differ from those encountered by physical embodied agents. A natural next step is therefore to extend Map2Vid toward real-world or mixed-reality data sources, and to study whether BEV-based control remains effective under stronger domain variability. Furthermore, while the framework effectively captures global navigation intent, fine-grained motion execution, such as precise angular orientation at sharp turns or subtle translational stability, remains a challenge, as evidenced by occasional errors in trajectory adherence observed in our qualitative analysis. Another constraint lies in the static nature of the current dataset: our formulation primarily focuses on fixed indoor layouts and does not yet account for dynamic obstacles or human-agent interactions, which are critical for comprehensive embodied intelligence. Finally, the reliance on large-scale Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) such as Wan2.2 and HunyuanVideo involves substantial computational overhead and inference latency, suggesting that future research into model distillation or efficient temporal attention mechanisms is necessary. [40] VII. C ONCLUSION 

In this work, we present Map2Vid , a novel framework that addresses the limitations of existing video generation models in providing intuitive and spatially grounded control for embodied navigation. By introducing Bird’s-Eye-View (BEV) maps as a human-friendly geometric condition, Map2Vid enables the synthesis of coherent egocentric videos that respect global scene layouts. A curriculum training strategy with progressive masking is adopted to regulate condition visibility, preventing shortcut learning and ensuring the model effectively internalizes the spatial dynamics encoded in the BEV maps. Through the construction of the large-scale Map2Vid-100K benchmark, it is found that our method supports flexible inference, achieving robust performance even when conditioned solely on BEV maps without explicit camera trajectories. A. Dataset Construction 

Modeling navigation across different floors introduces com-plex vertical transitions and spatial discontinuities, which are notoriously difficult for generative models to synthesize accurately. To ensure data consistency and focus on high-fidelity planar navigation, we strictly exclude trajectories involving cross-floor actions. Specifically, we employ a two-stage filtering mechanism consisting of floor segmentation and trajectory verification.  

> •

Floor Segmentation. We first initialize a floor detector that performs a bottom-up vertical scanning of the entire 3D scene. By analyzing the projected top-down views of the navigation mesh at varying altitudes, the detector computes pixel similarity scores between adjacent vertical slices. Significant deviations in similarity allow us to identify floor boundaries, thereby defining precise height intervals (coordinates along the Y -axis) for each distinct floor. > •

Trajectory Verification. For each candidate agent move-ment task, the system pre-loads the coordinates of all trajectory points. We utilize the pre-computed height interval database to assign a floor index to each point. The system iterates through the trajectory; if any two consecutive valid path points are detected to belong to different floor indices, the task is classified as a multi-floor action. Consequently, such tasks are marked as invalid and excluded from the final video generation pipeline.

C. Holistic Alignment via Full Control 

The quantitative superiority of the Full Control setting, particularly in perceptual metrics (FID, FVD), can be attributed to its ability to simultaneously constrain two manifolds in the latent space: 

Appearance Anchoring. In the map-only setting, the model is tasked with hallucinating 3D textures solely from a schematic layout. While the BEV map effectively dictates the structural topology (e.g., wall placements, room connections), it remains texturally ambiguous. This ambiguity forces the generative backbone to sample from a high-entropy distribution of possible appearances, often leading to temporal flickering and inconsistent texturing as the video progresses. The Full Control setting mitigates this by utilizing the First Frame 

and Text Instruction as strong semantic anchors. These inputs lock the generation into a specific subspace of the appearance manifold, resolving textural ambiguity. Consequently, the model can dedicate its geometric capacity—derived from the BEV map—to motion synthesis rather than texture hallucination. 

Geometric Grounding. Conversely, relying exclusively on semantic cues (common in standard T2V models) often results in “plausible but incorrect” navigation, where agents may traverse obstacles or violate spatial continuity. By integrating the BEV map and camera trajectory within the Full Control stack, we impose strict boundary conditions on the agent’s action space. Thus, Full Control does not merely provide 

more information; it creates a complementary constraint system where semantic priors ensure visual fidelity while geometric priors enforce physical plausibility. In this section, we provide more qualitative results to empirically validate the core premise of our proposed Map2Vid 

task: that BEV maps can serve as an effective, human-centric interface for controllable navigation video generation. D. The Optimization Paradox in Full Control 

Although Full Control provides richer conditioning signals, our ablation study reveals a potential optimization challenge: 

Modality Competency Collapse .DiT backbones pre-trained on large-scale video data tend to favor familiar text and image conditions. Under naive training (e.g., Constant Masking), the model may over-rely on these semantic cues while underutilizing the novel BEV signal, producing visually plausible videos with inaccurate navigation paths. Our curriculum masking strategy mitigates this issue by temporarily suppressing dominant semantic conditions, encour-aging the model to learn BEV-based geometric constraints before integrating all modalities.
