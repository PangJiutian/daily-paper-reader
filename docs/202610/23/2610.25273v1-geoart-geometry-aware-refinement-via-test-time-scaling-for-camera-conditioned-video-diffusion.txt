Abstract
Camera-conditioned video diffusion models often suffer
from inaccurate camera control and geometric inconsisten-
cies because camera constraints and estimated geometry
are only weakly enforced during denoising. Existing reme-
dies often require costly geometry-aware fine-tuning or ad-
ditional 3D supervision. We introduce GeoART, a training-
free self-refinement framework that improves camera con-
trollability through geometry-aware test-time scaling. At
selected denoising steps, GeoART generates multiple can-
didates within the Predict-and-Perturb [27] cycle and eval-
uates them using a geometry prior, without directly modify-
ing the denoising trajectory. A confidence gate suppresses
unreliable geometric evidence, while rank-calibrated prob-
abilistic selection combines score magnitude and relative
rank to choose the next refinement state.
Experiments
demonstrate consistent improvements in camera controlla-
bility, multi-view consistency, and visual quality over exist-
ing sampling and self-refinement methods, without training
or modifying the pretrained video diffusion model.
1. Introduction
Controllable video generation models have enabled camera-
guided novel-view synthesis [3, 18, 44, 64, 74, 81], interac-
tive world simulation [21, 23, 55, 66, 79], and embodied
navigation [7, 48, 75, 80]. In these applications, camera tra-
jectories or agent actions condition the generation of future
observations. Despite substantial progress, existing meth-
ods still suffer from camera misalignment, geometric in-
consistencies, and rendering artifacts. Even with 3D priors,
denoising operates in a spatiotemporal latent space without
a persistent 3D scene representation, limiting pose fidelity
and cross-view consistency under large viewpoint changes
and long-horizon generation.
To address these limitations, recent work has pursued
two complementary directions: (1) more effectively in-
tegrating 3D priors through geometry-aware condition-
ing [11, 22, 51, 70, 73], and (2) optimizing pretrained mod-
els with geometry-aware rewards to improve camera align-
ment and geometric consistency [1, 2, 32, 36, 71]. Despite
these advances, geometry-conditioned approaches often re-
quire large-scale training datasets with 3D supervision and
remain sensitive to errors in estimated geometry, whereas
reward-based approaches introduce a costly post-training
stage. Inference-time self-refinement offers a training-free
alternative but remains largely unexplored for improving
geometric consistency [5, 27, 58].
Existing methods re-
fine intermediate samples by steering them toward the data
manifold, improving semantic fidelity and physical plau-
sibility. However, their lack of geometry-aware feedback
limits improvements in camera controllability and geome-
try consistency. A natural extension is to incorporate ge-

ometric objectives through gradient-based diffusion guid-
ance [6, 9, 12, 45, 72]. However, balancing geometric con-
straints with the generative prior requires carefully tuned
guidance weights, while noisy or incomplete geometry can
misdirect the update and degrade generation quality.
These observations motivate a different use of geome-
try: as an evaluation signal rather than a direct intervention
in denoising. Inspired by verifier-based test-time scaling in
LLMs and diffusion models [10, 41, 46, 49, 53], we extend
Predict-and-Perturb self-refinement [27] by sampling multi-
ple candidates at selected perturbation stages and evaluating
their geometric consistency (Fig. 1). In this way, geometry
guides candidate selection without directly altering the la-
tent updates.
Based on this principle,
we propose GeoART, a
geometry-aware self-refinement framework for test-time
scaling.
GeoART scores candidate refinements using
confidence-aware geometric criteria and converts these
scores into a rank-calibrated sampling distribution. Rather
than greedily selecting the candidate with the highest raw
score, GeoART probabilistically chooses the next refine-
ment state based on both score magnitude and relative rank,
preserving exploration and reducing sensitivity to noisy
evaluations. By separating geometric evaluation from la-
tent optimization, GeoART improves camera adherence and
cross-view consistency without modifying or retraining the
underlying video diffusion model.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We introduce GeoART, a training-free framework that
formulates geometry-aware self-refinement as test-time
scaling, using geometry priors to evaluate local refine-
ment candidates rather than directly guide denoising.
• We develop a confidence-gated geometry evaluator that
suppresses unreliable geometric evidence when scoring
candidate refinements.
• We propose rank-calibrated probabilistic selection that
balances score magnitude and relative rank, improving
robustness to noisy geometry scores.
2. Related Work
2.1. Camera-Conditioned Video Diffusion
Recent video diffusion models enable controllable view-
point generation by conditioning on prescribed camera tra-
jectories [3, 4, 15, 18, 20, 31, 35, 64, 81, 82].
To im-
prove cross-view consistency, subsequent methods incorpo-
rate explicit geometric cues through depth or point-cloud
priors [11, 24, 51, 70, 73, 74, 77], 3D Gaussian Splatting
representations [16, 17, 37, 40, 44, 54], or geometry-aware
supervision [22, 42, 67, 76]. Another line of research is
geometry-aware alignment methods that improve consis-
tency using epipolar, reconstruction, or latent 3D rewards
for preference learning and reinforcement learning [1, 2,
13, 32, 62, 65]. VIGOR [71] further employs geometric
rewards for verifier-based test-time scaling.
In contrast,
GeoART performs geometry-aware test-time search locally
within self-refinement, without post-training or modifying
the underlying video diffusion model.
2.2. Self-Refined Sampling for Diffusion Models
Self-refinement allows a pretrained diffusion model to re-
visit intermediate states at inference time, typically by per-
turbing, inverting, or restarting denoising and regenerating
from the revised state. Existing approaches use inversion–
denoising cycles, restart sampling, reward-guided explo-
ration, or uncertainty-aware perturbations to escape subop-
timal trajectories and improve semantic fidelity and phys-
ical plausibility [5, 14, 27, 28, 47, 58, 69]. Complemen-
tary methods refine the initial noise or incorporate auxiliary-
model feedback to improve temporal coherence [33, 68].
Other approaches directly update intermediate samples us-
ing gradients from external objectives [6, 45, 72], including
epipolar, depth, and multi-view constraints [9, 12]. How-
ever, such guidance directly alters the denoising trajec-
tory and requires careful balancing of geometric constraints
against the generative prior. GeoART instead treats geom-
etry as an evaluator: it scores multiple self-refinement can-
didates and probabilistically selects the next state without
gradient-based geometric intervention.
2.3. Test-Time Scaling for Diffusion Models
Test-time scaling improves diffusion generation by allo-
cating additional inference computation to explore multi-
ple samples or denoising trajectories without further train-
ing.
Existing approaches use random search, particle-
based resampling, or tree search to identify trajectories
with higher verifier or reward scores [29, 46, 49, 52, 78].
EvoSearch [19] evolves populations of image and video
trajectories through iterative selection and mutation, while
Video-T1 [41] applies verifier-guided trajectory search to
video generation. However, these methods can incur sub-
stantial inference overhead and remain sensitive to ver-
ifier reliability.
Hard selection and pruning are brit-
tle under noisy scores, while commonly used perceptual
and semantic rewards provide limited geometric feedback.
GeoART instead performs periodic local branching within
self-refinement and combines confidence-aware geometric
evaluation with rank-calibrated probabilistic selection.

4.1. Geometry-Guided Test-Time Scaling
A standard P&P iteration generates only one random per-
turbation from the predicted clean latent. If this perturba-
tion leads toward an unfavorable region, subsequent iter-
ations may reinforce the resulting trajectory. This limita-
tion is particularly pronounced in camera-controlled video
generation, where the denoiser primarily promotes visual
quality and temporal coherence without explicitly enforc-
ing camera-motion or cross-frame geometry constraints.
We address this limitation through local test-time search.
Unlike conventional Best-of-N sampling, which generates
multiple complete videos [41, 46], GeoART branches the
trajectory only at selected solver steps. All branches origi-
nate from the same clean prediction and therefore preserve
similar semantic and appearance content while exploring
different local refinement directions.

4.2. Confidence-Gated Geometry Scoring
Reliable candidate evaluation requires a geometry prior
aligned with the target video trajectory. However, off-the-
shelf geometry estimators can be inaccurate in occluded or
textureless regions. Treating all geometric estimates equally
may therefore corrupt the score and lead to unreliable can-
didate selection.
Geometry-prior construction. Before diffusion sampling,
VGGT [59] processes the conditioning views {Iinput} to
reconstruct a scene point cloud and estimate corresponding
point-wise confidence. We project the reconstructed point
cloud and its confidence to the prescribed target-camera
poses {P cam
ℓ
}, producing a geometry-projected video and
a spatially aligned confidence video. Pixels without a valid
point projection, including missing and disoccluded re-
gions, are assigned zero confidence.
The projected point-cloud video is encoded once using
the frozen video VAE [30], producing the geometry latent g.
The confidence video is linearly interpolated to the spatial
and temporal resolution of g and broadcast across the latent
channels to obtain c. Pixels without valid projections are set
to zero. Consequently, g, c, and the diffusion latent share
the same dimensions. Both g and c are computed once and
reused throughout sampling.

4.3. Rank-Calibrated Soft Selection
Given N candidate scores, a direct strategy is to retain the
candidate with the highest score. However, this hard Best-
of-N decision can follow an incorrect refinement direction
when the geometry estimates are noisy or candidate scores
are similar. Applying a softmax directly to the raw scores
preserves exploration but remains sensitive to changes in
score scale across solver steps and scenes.
We therefore combine absolute score magnitude with
relative rank. Let S denote a random candidate score with
cumulative distribution function F(s) = Pr(S ≤s). For
a candidate with score s, the probability of outperforming
N −1 independent competitors is F(s)N−1. This order-
statistic interpretation motivates weighting each candidate
according to its empirical percentile within the current can-
didate set. Because F is unknown, we estimate it using
the self-inclusive empirical CDF and combine the rank
weighting with exponential score weighting:

5. Experiment
Implementation details.
We evaluate GeoART on two
camera-conditioned video diffusion backbones, Wan2.1-
Fun-Control-Camera [57] and Uni3C [11], to assess its gen-
eralizability across architectures. Both backbones are fine-
tuned on a combined training set of 800 sequences: 200
from DL3DV [38] and 600 from CO3D [50]. All frames are
resized to 256 × 256, and 33 frames are uniformly sampled
from each sequence to form a training clip. Each backbone
is fine-tuned for 20K iterations, requiring approximately 24
hours on a single GPU. GeoART is subsequently applied
without further training or model modification.
Evaluation protocol. We consider two levels of input spar-
sity: single-view conditioning and two-view head-and-tail
conditioning, where the first and last views are provided to
the video diffusion model. We evaluate all methods on 100
held-out scenes from CO3D and DL3DV using five random
seeds per scene. All metrics are averaged over the 500 gen-
erations. Each inference run requires approximately 30 GB
of GPU memory. Following Self-Refine [27], the refine-
ment schedule is R = {(5, 15, 2), (40, 45, 1)}, where each
tuple specifies the start step, end step, and number of re-
finement iterations, respectively. Additional implementa-
tion details are provided in the supplementary material.

struction fidelity using PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. We then
measure camera controllability using translation error (ET )

Candidate selection.
We compare our rank-calibrated
probabilistic selection with uniform, greedy, and score-only
alternatives. Uniform selection ignores geometry, whereas
greedy selection always chooses the highest-scoring can-
didate and is therefore sensitive to evaluation noise. Re-
moving rank calibration reduces the method to score-only
sampling, pk ∝exp(λsk), whose behavior depends on the
score scale. Our full strategy achieves the strongest over-
all performance by combining score magnitude with scale-
invariant relative rank, favoring geometrically consistent
candidates while retaining controlled exploration(Table 2,
Fig. 5).
Different geometry scoring models. We evaluate GeoART

rics(Table 2). Without the gate, unreliable evidence from
occluded, textureless, or poorly reconstructed regions con-

proving the robustness of geometry-aware refinement.
6. Conclusion
We introduced GeoART, a training-free geometry-aware
self-refinement framework that improves camera controlla-
bility and geometric consistency through test-time scaling.
GeoART uses geometry only to evaluate refinement candi-
dates, while confidence-gated scoring and rank-calibrated
probabilistic selection reduce sensitivity to unreliable es-
timates. Experiments across two backbones demonstrate
consistent gains in camera alignment, multi-view consis-
tency, and visual quality without retraining. Future work
will focus on reducing inference cost and improving geom-
etry estimation for videos with moving objects.

6.1. More Implementation Details
Common evaluation setup.
We evaluate all baselines
using the same trained Wan2.1-Fun-V1.1-1.3B-Control-
Camera [57] and Uni3C [11] camera-conditioned video-
generation checkpoints under both one-view and two-view
settings. In the one-view setting, a single input image con-
ditions image-to-video generation.
In the two-view set-
ting, the first and last views serve as head-and-tail condi-
tions for video interpolation. Unless otherwise specified,
each generated video contains 33 frames at a spatial reso-
lution of 256 × 256. To improve precise camera control,
we fine-tune both models on combination of CO3D and
DL3DV. Each ground-truth clip spans 40 source frames,
from which 33 frames are randomly sampled for training.
Camera poses are expressed relative to the first camera, and
per-pixel Pl¨ucker ray embeddings are computed from the
corresponding camera intrinsics and extrinsics. All meth-
ods use identical prompts, conditioning observations, cam-
era trajectories, model checkpoints, and random seeds. We
use seed 0,10,20,30,40 and evaluate 50 videos from each
dataset. Sampling is performed in bfloat16 using a flow-
matching scheduler with a shift parameter of 5 and tiled
VAE decoding. The follwing are implementation details of
other baselines.

standard sampler provided by each video backbone. At ev-

combines the conditional and unconditional predictions as

use an ℓ1 prediction-difference threshold of 0.1 and termi-

99.9% of latent locations are considered converged. This
baseline does not require an external reward model or addi-

Z-Sampling.
We implement Zigzag Diffusion Sampling
(Z-Sampling) following [5]. The standard denoising trajec-
tory is augmented with a backward–forward refinement dur-
ing the first 40 of the 50 sampling steps. At each selected
step, we first perform the ordinary strongly guided denois-
ing update. The resulting latent is then approximately in-
verted toward the preceding noise level using weak guid-
ance γweak = 0, after which it is denoised again using
strong guidance γstrong = 5. The second denoised result
replaces the original update. This gives 50 ordinary transi-
tions and 80 additional zigzag transitions, for a total of 130
trajectory evaluations.


Video-T1.
We implement the linear-search variant of
Video-T1 [41]. For each input, we independently gener-
ate N = 10 complete videos using random seeds s and
s + 1, with 50 solver steps per candidate. Each candidate
is evaluated using the released VisionReward-Video veri-
fier. The verifier uniformly samples 24 frames from the
generated video and evaluates the released set of prompt-
conditioned binary quality questions. The binary answers
are mapped to +1 and −1, weighted using the released
question weights, and averaged. We retain the candidate
with the higher prompt-conditioned weighted score.
We
use only candidate-level linear search and do not employ
the Tree-of-Frames search variant.
VIGOR.
For VIGOR [71], we use the same best-of-N
protocol with N = 10, but replace the semantic verifier
with the released geometry-oriented reward.
Each can-
didate is evaluated using the official VGGT-based point-
wise reprojection-error evaluator, and the candidate with the
lower reprojection error is selected. We use the default re-
leased VIGOR configuration and disable the optional sky-
region filter. The generator is not fine-tuned; VIGOR is
used exclusively as an inference-time path verifier.
GC-Ref.
We adapt Geometric Consistency Refinement
(GC-Ref) [9] to video by optimizing only the initial diffu-
sion noise while keeping the generator weights, condition-
ing observations, and camera parameters frozen. We first
generate an initial video and use the RoMa matcher in the
paper to establish dense correspondences between the first
conditioning image and selected generated frames. The ref-
erence pixel locations and confidence mask are fixed from
this initial prediction, while the corresponding target loca-
tions are recomputed differentiably during optimization.
The refinement objective is
LGC-Ref = Lepi + λRGBLRGB,
(16)
where Lepi is a Huber penalty on the symmetric point-
to-epipolar-line distance, and LRGB is the ℓ1 color differ-
ence between matched pixels. We use five Adam optimiza-
tion iterations, a learning rate of 0.025, a RoMa confidence
threshold of 0.15, λRGB = 2.5, a Huber threshold of 2.0, a
temporal frame stride of 4, and at most two target frames.
Each optimization iteration performs a complete 50-step de-
noising trajectory with CFG scale 3. After optimization, a
final trajectory is generated from the refined initial noise.

6.6. Failure Cases
GeoART has two main failure modes. First, it inherits er-
rors from the geometry estimator. In textureless, reflective,
transparent, or heavily occluded regions, VGGT may pro-
duce inaccurate geometry or camera correspondences. Al-
though confidence gating suppresses uncertain predictions,
it cannot remove errors assigned high confidence. Such er-
rors can bias candidate selection and cause warped struc-
tures, duplicated details, or temporal flickering.
Second, GeoART searches over a finite, randomly gen-
erated candidate pool. With N = 5, the pool may not con-
tain a suitable update at a difficult denoising step. Conse-
quently, GeoART may preserve the overall geometry and
camera alignment while sacrificing fine texture quality. To-
gether, inaccurate geometry estimates and limited candi-
date coverage account for the primary failure cases of our
method(Figure 7).
6.7. Limitations
The proposed method depends on a pretrained geometry es-
timator and assumes that camera intrinsics and extrinsics
are available or can be estimated reliably. Its performance


is consequently bounded by the accuracy and domain gen-
eralization of VGGT. The confidence threshold is also se-
lected heuristically, and a fixed threshold may not transfer
optimally to scenes with different image statistics, motion
patterns, or camera baselines.
Geometry-guided test-time scaling increases inference
cost relative to standard diffusion sampling. These opera-
tions increase runtime and memory consumption, limiting
the practicality of the current implementation for extremely
long or high-resolution videos.
Future work could replace dataset-specific thresholds
with learnable uncertainty calibration, incorporate tempo-
ral memory and dynamic-scene geometry, and use adaptive
candidate allocation so that additional computation is con-
centrated only at uncertain denoising steps.
