Abstract. We consider the problem of amortized Bayesian inference for nonlinear inverse prob-lems in settings where only samples from the joint distribution of parameters and observations are available. Our framework also covers function-space inverse problems, where the unknown parameter belongs to a Banach space of functions. Classical approaches such as Markov chain Monte Carlo require solution of a new inference problem for each observation; this can be computationally pro-hibitive when posterior inference must be repeated for many different observations, especially when the parameter space is infinite-dimensional. Amortized Bayesian inversion addresses this issue by learning, in an offline training stage, a single map that can rapidly produce posterior samples for new observations. We propose a transport-based approach to amortized Bayesian inversion that learns an observation-dependent transport map that pushes forward a reference measure to approximate the posterior distribution, conditioned on the observation that is an input to the map. The model is trained by minimizing an averaged energy-distance objective between the true posterior and the learned pushforward. The averaging over observations allows for generalization across observation instances and efficient amortized inference. Furthermore, the formulation is likelihood-free, requir-ing only samples from the joint distribution and avoiding explicit evaluation of the likelihood. In addition, the use of an energy-distance objective removes the need for invertibility of the transport map and for computation of Jacobian determinants, enabling flexible parameterizations in high- and infinite-dimensional settings. Moreover, when the posterior is assumed to have density with respect to a Gaussian prior measure, we construct transport maps that take the form of the identity plus a learnable map taking values in the Cameron–Martin space of the prior. This guarantees that the learned posterior remains absolutely continuous with respect to the prior. In infinite dimensions, the transport map is parameterized using neural operators, enabling use at different grid resolutions. We illustrate the approach on a finite-dimensional problem and then consider two PDE-based inverse problems that arise in porous medium flow and seismic inversion. The results show that the learned transport accurately captures the posterior structure, including multimodality and dominant modes, while providing fast posterior sampling compared to traditional methods. 

Key words. Bayesian inverse problems; Amortized inference; Likelihood-free inference; Trans-port maps; Infinite-dimensional inverse problems; Cameron–Martin space; Energy distance; Neural operators.

1. Introduction. Inverse problems arise in many areas of science and engineer-ing [24, 11, 26], where one seeks to infer unknown parameter u ∈ U from indirect and noisy observation y ∈ Y . Examples include the estimation of unknown fields and model parameters in mechanics, medical imaging, and geophysics, where the available data are typically sparse, noisy, and indirectly related to the quantities of interest [15, 20, 1, 14, 16]. These challenges motivate probabilistic formulations of inverse problems that quantify the uncertainty in the inferred parameters rather than producing a single deterministic estimate. A common mathematical formulation is based on the stochastic forward model: (1.1) y = G(u, η ),

where G : U × E → Y denotes the forward operator, mapping parameter u and noise η to the observation. In many applications, posterior inference must be performed not for a single observation, but repeatedly for many different realizations of the data. This situation arises, for example, when analyzing many experimental configurations in experimental design [12] or many synthetic and observational data sets in uncertainty quantification, design, and real-time inference [23, 13]. Classical sampling methods such as Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) [8], and infinite dimensional variants [10], require the solution of a new inverse problem for each observation y†. This repeated online cost can become computationally prohibitive. This motivates amortized Bayesian inference. The central idea is to replace re-peated, observation-specific inference with a reusable model learned during an offline training stage, which may be applied with any observation. Once trained, this model can be evaluated rapidly for any observation y†, producing approximate posterior samples without evaluation of the likelihood multiple times as would be required to deploy MCMC. Thus, amortization is particularly attractive when inference must be repeated many times for different observations. After training, posterior samples for a new observation y† are obtained by drawing samples from the reference measure and evaluating the learned map Tθ (· ; y†) on those samples. Measure transport provides a natural framework for representing compli-cated probability distributions as pushforwards of simpler reference measures [27, 18]. In Bayesian inference, transport maps have been used to approximate posterior dis-tributions by constructing deterministic couplings between a tractable reference mea-sure and the target posterior [19]. Many existing approaches are based on objectives involving the Kullback–Leibler divergence [4, 9]. However, such formulations often require the map to be invertible and require evaluation of Jacobian determinants. These requirements are restrictive in high-dimensional problems and become espe-cially problematic in infinite-dimensional settings. Alternative approaches seek trans-ports by minimizing optimal transport distances [3, 5, 25], often requiring adversarial optimization, which is challenging to scale to high-dimensional settings. We address the limitations of existing transport-based methodologies for amor-tized inference by training the transport map using an averaged energy-distance objec-tive between the true posterior and the learned pushforward distribution. In contrast, our approach trains a direct pushforward map by mini-mizing an energy-distance discrepancy between posterior measures, without requiring invertibility, density evaluation, Jacobian determinants, or divergence computations. When the parameter u is a function, this transport-map perspective must also re-spect the measure-theoretic structure of Bayesian inverse problems in function space. In typical Bayesian inverse problems, the posterior measure is absolutely continuous with respect to the prior [24]; in particular, the posterior and prior cannot be mu-tually singular. In infinite-dimensional problems, it is a delicate issue to avoid such mutual singularity. For example, Gaussian measures are mutually singular unless the conditions of the Feldman-Hajek theorem are met [11, Theorem 37]. We address this issue in the context of inverse problems in Banach space, with Gaussian priors. We construct transport maps that respect the measure-theoretic structure of Gaussian priors on function spaces [6, 7]. 2. Methodology. This section introduces the proposed amortized transport methodology. Section 2.1 defines the averaged energy-distance objective used to learn conditional pushforward approximations of the posterior distributions, derives a sample-based form involving only the joint distribution of parameters and obser-vations, and relates the objective to the energy score. Section 2.2 then describes the transport-map architecture; in the setting of an unknown parameter in a Hilbert space, the architecture is further constrained by means of a Cameron–Martin-informed construction that is natural when Gaussian priors are adopted on the function space.

Variable θ denotes the parameters of a neural network or neural operator employed to define Tθ (· ; y). A key aspect of our approach is the use of the energy distance to quantify the discrepancy between distributions, resulting in the objective function defined by Eq. (1.2). In contrast to approaches based on the Kullback–Leibler divergence, this choice does not require the transport map to be invertible, nor does it require evalu-ation of Jacobian determinants. Since the energy distance is naturally com-patible with sample-based approximation, this representation enables training using only samples from the joint distribution, without requiring explicit knowledge of the prior or the likelihood. 2.2. Parameterization of the Transport Map. To complete the formula-tion, we specify the class of transport maps Tθ . In finite dimensions, we use standard deep neural network architectures. But while the objective function applies to general measurable maps, the infinite-dimensional setting imposes structural constraints that we build into the architecture via inductive bias. Since the posterior is absolutely con-tinuous with respect to the prior, it is desirable that the approximation preserves this property. In infinite-dimensional spaces, this is restrictive: arbitrary perturbations of 

u can lead to pushforward measures that are singular with respect to the prior. We focus on Gaussian priors, and the detailed background on this topic may be found in Appendix A. U into itself for each fixed y in the support of κ, this construction ensures that the perturbation lies in the Cameron–Martin space. 3. Numerical Experiments. This section evaluates the proposed amortized transport methodology on a sequence of inverse problems of increasing complexity. The purpose of these experiments is to illustrate the main ideas of the proposed methodology rather than to provide a comprehensive benchmarking study. The prob-lems are not at the edge of computational feasibility, but rather are chosen to be tractable via MCMC methodology in order to demonstrate the benefits of our ap-proach in comparison. In particular, we show that: (i) use of the energy-distance objective leads to efficient observation-dependent posterior approximations that out-perform MCMC when multiple observation instances are encountered; and that (ii) the Cameron–Martin-informed parameterization is beneficial in function-space set-tings, analogously to the benefits of function-space formulations of MCMC. We consider a finite-dimensional nonlinear example with a tractable reference posterior, followed by two PDE-based inverse problems defined by Darcy flow and wave propagation respectively. In the Darcy problem, we recover a log-normal per-meability field, and in the wave propagation problem, a piecewise constant wavespeed field, defined via thresholding a Gaussian random field. 4. Conclusion. We have proposed an amortized transport-based approach for Bayesian inverse problems; furthermore, we have proposed specific adaptations of the approach that are relevant when the unknown parameter belongs to an infinite-dimensional function space. The method learns a conditional transport map that pushes forward a reference distribution to approximate the posterior distribution cor-responding to a given observation. The map is trained by minimizing an averaged, over observations, energy-distance objective, which can be expressed using samples from the joint distribution of parameters and observations.
